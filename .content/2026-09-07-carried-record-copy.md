## 0 · Two decisions underneath all five patterns

### ⭐ 0a — Rule 5 inverts the default. Nothing arrives already done.

The map is blank when she reaches it, by ruling. So **the carried-over notice (§1) is not the
dominant pattern — it is the exception**, dormant unless Paul rules a specific item across. The
dominant work is §2: inviting her to give again what she already gave, without that reading as a debt,
a loss, or a test.

### ⭐ 0b — The origin we name is the OCCASION, not the mechanism.

She said these things at a table, in a note, out loud. That — not a frozen instance, a KV namespace or
an archive — is where they came from *in the only frame she has*. So anything traceable to her says
**who said it, when, and where she was standing**, and never says *migrated · imported · carried over ·
the old app · the previous version*.

Naming the mechanism would be the surface explaining a system she does not know about, at her — the
exact shape Paul ruled out twice on 2026-09-06. The mechanism is not hidden: it has its own door in
**§4**, opened by her question rather than by our need to explain. ⛔ Where the occasion is not known
truthfully, print the date alone and say nothing about where.

### ⚠️ The reader fact that decides §2 and §3

`.private/mom-feedback-2026-07-26.md`, in her own words and unprompted: **she will ask; she will not
answer.** Asking is safe, answering is exposure, and the mechanism is her fear of being wrong — not
disengagement, reading difficulty or modality. [measured] Corroborated by the standing split:
**0-for-35 on affordances that ask her, 5-for-5 on the one that simply moves her** [measured — lap 8].

⭐ **This is why rule 5 is good copy as well as good method.** A blank map she names is *telling*; a
pre-filled map she confirms is *answering*. The ruling moves the whole zone conversation out of the
register she does not use and into the one she does. Say so to Paul — it is a reason to hold the line
when the empty map feels unhelpful in the room.

## 1 · PATTERN A — the carried-over notice `[RARE — gated per item]`

> **Register: orienting.** Not celebratory, not a receipt.

*Rationale: the notice and the credit are the same line — naming who said it and when IS the
acknowledgment, so no thank-you or receipt is needed anywhere (`credit, don't thank`).*

⛔ **Precondition, and it is the whole of this section's discipline: nothing carries by default.** Under
rule 5 an item appears in her new household only where **Paul has ruled that specific item across, and
the ruling is in the register**. This copy exists so that a ruled-across item has correct words — never
so that having good words becomes a reason to carry something. *A ruling that is not in the register is
not in force* (`BACKLOG.md`:134).

**Live candidates, so the pattern is not hypothetical:** the Track B vehicles/equipment material whose
WORK freeze is lifted [measured — rule 2], and the one September 6 note nothing has answered.

### A1 · The one-line label, beside an item `[SURFACE] · DRAFT`

| what the item is | label |
|---|---|
| a name she gave a thing | `Your name for it, August 30.` |
| something she told me | `You told me this, August 20.` |
| her name over my drawing or write-up | `Your name, August 30. My drawing.` |
| ⚠️ something she said out loud | **not drafted** — the true verb depends on the recording; Paul supplies it. |

⭐ **The label carries origin and date. It does NOT carry changeability — a control does.** `Change ›`
beside the item does the whole job. A *sentence* saying "you can change this" repeated beside several
items is the standing footer CLAUDE.md #5 forbids, and the repeated reassurance that reads as a warning
that something is fragile. Same call Paul already made when `Change it if you'd rather` was cut because
`Change that ›` sat one line below.

⛔ **No carried notice ships onto a surface with no control beside it.** A notice saying *this came from
you* with no way to change it is a notice that the thing is stuck — and the reader it teaches that to is
the one whose stated fear is being wrong. Placement requirement → §7.

### A2 · The short card, a module's first open `[SURFACE] · DRAFT`

**Template — three lines, the empty-card brief's shape:**

```
{whose} {what}, {occasion}, {date}
{what of it is mine, if the item is a mix}
{control}
```

**A ruled-across item (worked example — the September 6 note):**

> This is one I hadn't answered yet.
>
> **In your words, September 6**
> *"{herWords}"*
> `Anything to add ›`

⛔ **`{herWords}` renders verbatim from the server's stored value, never local state, never touched by a
model.** No cleanup, no sentence case, no added period; absent value → whole block removed. ⛔ **No
reporting verb in front of it** — not *"You said:"*, not *"You told me:"*. The heading orients; a
reporting verb puts a narrator between her and her own sentence.

### A3 · Two calls inside this pattern

- ⛔ **No count.** *"Sixteen names are yours"* is credit and it is true — and it teaches the surface to
  render her contribution as a tally, one short step from rendering what is *left* as one.
- ⚠️ **A weekday, if used, is derived from the stored timestamp — never typed.** *"That Sunday
  evening"* helps her find the memory; a wrong weekday on the one line claiming to know when she spoke
  is the cheapest way to lose this reader.

## 2 · PATTERN B — the re-collection invitation, and the acknowledgment `[DOMINANT]`

> **Register: inviting.** The door is open. Nothing is owed by her.

*Rationale: asking someone to give again what they already gave is the single most dangerous act in
this transition — it can only be safe if the first giving is credited, kept, and named as not-lost
before the ask arrives.*

### B1 · The re-collection invitation — the map opens empty `[SPOKEN] · DRAFT`

> "You named these places for me back in August — I wrote every one down and I've still got them.
> We're starting this one empty because I'd rather build it with you than hand you mine."

Four beats in one breath: **credited** (you named them) · **kept** (I still have them) · **why it's
empty** (a choice, not a loss, not her fault) · **the invitation** (build it with you). It never thanks,
never grades, never says *again*.

**On-surface counterpart `[SURFACE] · DRAFT` — short form:**

> Empty on purpose — this one gets built with you.

**On-surface counterpart — with the credit carried durably (recommended, see B3):**

> The names you gave me in August are kept. This map starts fresh.

⛔ **Never say "again."** *"Tell me again what you call this"* converts her August evening into wasted
effort in three words. The word is barred outright on these surfaces.

⛔ **Never explain the comparison to her.** The 23 zones are an answer key and the divergence is the
measurement [measured — rule 5 (ii)]. Telling her that turns naming into a test, on the surface where
the whole design exists to keep her out of the answering register. See ⛔ RULE #3 — it is Paul's, and
my recommendation is: she is told the names are kept, and never that they are being scored against.

### B2 · The debt line, and the ask that follows it `[SURFACE] · DRAFT`

Appears **only in a module that actually holds something unanswered**; where nothing is held there is no
card (the degradation rule — a missing slot removes its line, never leaves a gap).

> Some of what you told me I haven't answered yet — it comes back as each part opens.

**The disclosure sentence, attached to the ask beneath it:**

> Only used for your own record, seen only by me, and you can change any of it later.

Four required halves in one calm sentence — **use** (*for your own record*) · **not-use** (*only*, which
carries it) · **who sees it** (*seen only by me*) · **reversibility** (*change any of it later*). 19
words at A+. [`feedback_every_ask_says_use_and_reversibility`, extended 2026-09-05]

⭐ **This reversibility clause is NOT the "everything is changeable" scaffolding and must not be counted
against its one-instance budget.** Two jobs: the scaffolding is *reassurance about her* and decays on
Paul's read; a disclosure's reversibility half is *a fact about the ask* and never decays. Collapsing
them yields either a standing footer or an ask that quietly drops a required disclosure. (Proposed to
the library — §10.)

**The door:** `Anything to add ›`

### B3 · ⭐ Z-ACK — the acknowledgment that is not a rendering of her map

Rule 5 removes the acknowledgment that was going to happen by construction: she will never see her 23
zones rendered back, and **16 of those names are hers** [measured — rule 5, ⚠️ block]. Three candidate
forms, ordered, with a recommendation.

**Z1 `[SPOKEN]` — in the room, at the moment the blank map opens.** That is B1 above, unchanged.
⚠️ **Necessary and not sufficient.** An acknowledgment that exists only in speech is not in the register
— the same failure this whole block was written to fix — and this reader reads with difficulty and may
not retain a sentence said once over a laptop.

**Z2 `[SURFACE]` — one durable line at the empty map.** The second B1 counterpart above. One line, zero
build, and it makes the acknowledgment re-readable. **Ship this whatever else is decided.**

**Z3 ⭐ `[SURFACE]` — a place to look back at what she has told me, in her words, off the map.**
**Recommended, and it is what she asked for in her own words:** *"Is there a way to look back at these,
eg in the 'journal'?"* — 2026-07-26, 9:01 AM, unprompted, already quoted in the public backlog.
[measured] The deferred *"What you've settled"* surface was held pending engagement signal; **this is
that signal, and it is from the reader herself.**

**DRAFT — its face:**

> **What you've told me**
> In your words, oldest last.
> `{date} — "{herWords}"` *(repeating; verbatim, no reporting verb, no grading)*

⚠️ **The rule-5 tension, named rather than assumed away.** Is a view of her own past words a
*prepopulation*? **My read: no, and the boundary is drawable.** Rule 5 protects **her work** from
arriving already done — the map, the zones, the record she would otherwise build. A notebook of things

## 3 · PATTERN C — "is this right?" for a name I wrote down

> **Register: confirming.** Describe, don't grade. Her word stays hers, exactly as she said it.

*Rationale: the possible error goes on my side of the line, never hers — for a reader whose fear is
getting words wrong, "did you mean…?" is an accusation and "I may have written it down wrong" is the
same question with the risk moved to the person who can afford it.*

Under rule 5 this pattern **moves off the screen and into the room**: the names she gives while the map
is being built together are written down by Paul, so the confirmation is a read-back at the table, not
a card. That is ruling B's shape — *her words, my transcription*.

### C1 `[SPOKEN]` — the read-back, in the moment · DRAFT

> "Let me read that back to you — is that how you'd say it?"

And, once, when the writing-down starts:

> "I'll write these the way you say them, not the way I'd say them."

⭐ **Recommend ruling B on the evidence, not just on build cost.** A tap-confirm is an *answer*, which
is the register she is 0-for-35 in and which she has told us in her own words is exposure. A read-back
at the table is *asking*, which is the register she uses. The near-term build for B is nothing new; the
reader argument is the stronger of the two.

### C2 `[SURFACE]` — the on-screen form, only if ruling B goes the other way · DRAFT

> Is this the right name?
> `[ Yes, that's it ✓ ]`  `[ Not quite ]`

`Yes, that's it` is the only filled + ✓ control; `Not quite` is the outlined neutral — standing rule 1,
one affirmative grammar everywhere she taps. `Not quite` opens the existing confirm-screen box worded
**`What should it say?`** — reuse the string, don't mint a second.

### C3 · The gate on the sixteen names

⛔ **The 16 August names were read off Paul's hand-annotation by vision — a MODEL READ, unverified until
Paul confirms each name** [measured — `.plans/2026-08-31-zones-traced-with-mom.json`
`_meta.captured.transcription`]. Under rule 5 they never render to her, which lowers the display risk to
zero — **and raises the spoken risk**, because the likeliest way an unverified name reaches her is now
Paul reading one aloud from that list in the room. ⛔ **No name is spoken to her off the unverified
list.** Confirm against the annotated image first, or say nothing about which names were given.

### C4 · Forbidden here specifically

`Did you mean…?` · `We think you said…` · `Confirm your zone names` · `Please verify` · `Is this
correct?` · any tick/cross glyph used as a verdict on her word · any normalisation of her word (sentence
case, added article, "improved" phrasing). **If she named it, that is its name.**
⚠️ **A name she corrects is not a name that was wrong** — the mistake, if any, is in my handwriting or my
reading of it, and no line may print otherwise.

## 5 · PATTERN E — the register, the channel, and the rules

### E1 · Register and channel by surface

| surface | register | channel | what she is doing |
|---|---|---|---|
| carried label · carried card (§1) | **orienting** | surface | opening something and checking whether it is hers |
| re-collection invitation (B1) | **inviting** | **spoken**, + one surface counterpart | sitting down to build the map |
| debt line + ask (B2) | **inviting** | surface | deciding whether to say more |
| Z-ACK look-back (B3 · Z3) | **orienting** | surface | looking back at what she gave |
| read-back (C1) · confirm (C2) | **confirming** | **spoken** (C1) / surface (C2) | hearing her own word back |
| stayed-behind (D1) · old page (D2) | **orienting** | either — answers a question | she asked; the answer orients and stops |

**Voice does not move across these. The register does.**

### E2 · ⭐ The spoken/surface rule

The guided visit means Paul's mouth is temporarily a product surface. Two constraints follow:

1. **⛔ Every spoken line either has an on-surface counterpart, or is explicitly marked visit-only and
   recorded as not generalising.** `PRODUCT-ENGINE.md`'s stated end goal is *a product that does not run
   through Paul's mouth*; a transition whose acknowledgments live only in speech builds the dependency
   it is meant to retire. B1 and D2(iii) have counterparts drafted above; C1 and D2(ii) are **visit-only
   by design** and are marked so here rather than left to be discovered.
2. **A spoken line is written to be said, not read.** Contractions, no headings, no bullet grammar, one
   breath. A sentence Paul has to perform will come out as a paraphrase, and a paraphrase of an
   acknowledgment is not an acknowledgment.

### E3 · ⛔ Forbidden words on these surfaces

**Mechanism vocabulary** — *migrated · imported · data · sync/synced · transfer/transferred · version ·
instance · estate · household* (schema words) *· backup · restore · archive · the old app · the previous
version ·* ⚠️ ***carried over***. That last is this brief's own internal name for §1 and is fine for us;
on her screen it is mechanism-first and fails §0b.

**Task-manager vocabulary** — *due · overdue · pending · outstanding · items · queue · backlog · task ·
action required · review · submit · verify · complete/incomplete · remaining · N of M · awaiting.*
(Mom-derived bans, not a house style — do not lift this list onto a surface with a different reader.)

**Our words for her words** — *feedback · input · submission · entry · record* (as a noun for what she
gave). Name the thing: *your name for it*, *what you told me*, *your note*.

**And one word of ours:** ⛔ ***again***, in any re-collection context (B1).

### E4 · ⛔ What never appears, in any wording

1. **A count of anything unanswered** — not in a badge, chip, heading, sentence or sort order.
2. **Anything implying she owes work** — *you still need to · waiting on you · when you get a chance ·
   your turn · unfinished.*
3. **Anything that thanks her.** No *thanks*, no *we appreciate*, no praise of her contribution (*good
   catch*, *great question*, *helpful*). **Credit instead:** attach her name to the content.
4. **A completeness promise about reading or answering.** Keeping may be promised (D2 iii); reading may
   not. The 63 swept arrivals make every reading-claim false.
5. **A repeated apology for the wait.** Named once, as a fact (B2), never again.
6. **A standing "you can change this" footer.** A control does that job.
7. **Any statement of the comparison** between her old names and her new ones (B1).
8. **A weekday, date or channel not derived from a stored value.**

```
## A disclosure's reversibility clause is not the changeable-scaffolding clause  [agent-proposed 2026-09-07]
**Statement**: "You can change this later" does two different jobs — the reversibility half of a
use/who-sees-it disclosure attached to an ask, and standing reassurance to a reader still learning that
her input is safe. Count them separately. The first never decays and may not be cut; the second is
scaffolding, budgeted, and retires on the owner's read.
**Why**: Fernwood 2026-09-07. CLAUDE.md #5 caps "everything is changeable" at one varied instance and
forbids a standing footer; the standing ask rule requires every ask to state reversibility. Read as one
rule they contradict — either the disclosure loses a required half or the scaffolding becomes a footer.
Two rules about two sentences that happen to share a verb.
**Avoid**: Spending the scaffolding budget on a disclosure. Cutting a disclosure's reversibility half
because "we already said that once."
```

```
## Re-collection is only safe after credit, keeping, and a reason  [agent-proposed 2026-09-07]
**Statement**: When you ask someone to give again something they already gave, three things must be
said before the ask: that they gave it, that it is kept, and why you are starting fresh anyway. Miss any
one and the invitation reads as a debt, a loss, or a test. ⛔ The word "again" is barred — it prices
their first giving at zero.
**Why**: Fernwood 2026-09-07, `BACKLOG.md` rule 5 — the map arrives empty by ruling, so the reader is
asked to re-name places she named at a kitchen table five weeks earlier. The reader's documented reason
for not answering questions is fear of being wrong; an uncredited re-ask reads as *the first answers
weren't good enough.*
**When it applies**: Migrations, rebuilds, re-onboarding, any second pass at user-supplied content, any
"let's start over" where the first pass was real work.
**Avoid**: "Tell me again…" · framing the fresh start as a system limitation rather than a choice ·
explaining that the first pass is being used as a benchmark — that converts giving into being scored.
```

```
## Name the occasion, not the mechanism  [agent-proposed 2026-09-07 — pending Paul's read]
**Statement**: When copy tells a reader where something of theirs came from, name the moment they
remember — where they were, what they were doing, when — not the system that moved it. Keep the
mechanism available as an answer to a question, never as a broadcast.
**Why**: Fernwood 2026-09-07. The true mechanical answer ("your old instance") introduces a system the
reader has no concept of, on the screen where she is checking whether something of hers landed — and it
is less true to her experience than "that evening at the table," which is where it actually came from.
**Avoid**: Migration vocabulary on a reader-facing surface. Guessing an occasion — where it is not known
truthfully, print the date alone and say nothing about where.
```
