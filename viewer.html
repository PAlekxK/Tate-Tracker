On the property
Below the seventeen plants the card tends, the land itself is a Mesic Cove and Montane Oak Forest mosaic at 2,959 feet. What grows here naturally — and what is missing — sits in this section.
Rare species worth looking for
GA DNR lists these rich-cove species as worth noticing if you find them on the property — special-concern, not yet endangered: cucumber-root, galax, trailing arbutus, partridge-berry, round-leaved violet. In wetter seepage spots: umbrella leaf, turk's-cap lily, bee balm, Canadian wood nettle, and several orchids including the Pink Lady's Slipper. Look, don't pick. Full species accounts and habitat associations are in the GA DNR Natural Communities PDF.
This forest was once chestnut canopy
The American chestnut was a dominant canopy tree across the southern Appalachians until the chestnut blight, introduced from Asia in 1904, killed virtually all mature trees by 1950. At this elevation it would have been one of the most important nut producers — turkey, bear, squirrel, deer — and a major timber tree. The American Chestnut Foundation's Restoration Chestnut 1.0 (released 2005) brings blight-resistant trees back to participating landowners through regional chapters.
Hemlocks at this elevation
Eastern and Carolina hemlocks are being lost to the Hemlock Woolly Adelgid, statewide in Georgia since 2012. The treatment that works is an imidacloprid or dinotefuran soil drench applied at root flare, anytime the ground isn't frozen or saturated — a 2015 multi-state trial showed 96% survival of treated hemlocks at four years versus 48% of untreated. The GA Forestry Commission documents the treatment protocol; the Hemlock Restoration Initiative coordinates regionally.
Sourcing
For restorative plantings, wild-collected stock drives some natives toward extinction; ethically-propagated nurseries are the only acceptable path for rare species. The State Botanical Garden of Georgia and the Georgia Native Plant Initiative publish a recommended-nurseries list (June 2025). Nearby: Nearly Native (Fayetteville), North Georgia Native Plant Nursery, and Gardens of the Blue Ridge (NC) for hard-to-find Appalachian wildflowers.
