# Reference card — voice review

**Project:** Fernwood
**Surface:** Proposed new dashboard card surfacing `research-resources.md` as a reader-visible reference shelf
**Audience:** Paul (curator); Mom (half-engaged morning reader) — same primary/secondary as the rest of the dashboard
**Charter applied:** `~/.claude/content-principles/fernwood.md` + `cross-project.md`
**Touchstone:** *A Sand County Almanac* (Leopold)
**Mode:** Review (naming + framing decisions, no copy shipped)
**Could-be-anyone test:** drives all five recommendations
**Anchor check:** drives recommendation 1 specifically

---

## Punch list — Paul's five questions, with my recommendation per

1. **Card title — recommend "The Library."** Form-layer name, almanac-coded without being coy, and survives growth.
2. **Subtitle — recommend "What we read to learn this place."** First choice; two alternatives below.
3. **Category-section labels — keep them utilitarian, but rewrite the eight existing headers from utilitarian-clinical to utilitarian-plain.** Voice slip is real, but a coded set ("Plant lore," "Wildlife counts") would fail the could-be-anyone test for a reference shelf.
4. **Per-entry framing — keep Paul's existing prose. Don't rewrite. Only patch entries where the *first sentence* slips into ad-copy register.** Same anchor-the-first-sentence pattern as the plant-guide audit.
5. **"Running list" aspect — implicit truth, no words.** A growing reference shelf doesn't announce itself; that's almanac-form-fluent and doesn't need acknowledgment.

Detail and trade-offs below.

## F1 — Card title — recommend "The Library"

**Severity:** critical (this is the headline naming decision)

**Voice principle invoked:** *Anchored naming beats field-journal-fluent naming* (Fernwood charter, 2026-05-20). The form-layer-name-beats-voice-layer-name precedent from the Almanac rename.

### The dual-frame logic, applied

The "Field Notes → The Almanac" pass taught two things:

1. **Form-layer beats voice-layer for a UI surface.** "Field Notes" was the voice-layer name (what the *content* is); "The Almanac" is the form-layer name (what the *reader* is reading). Form-layer won because that's what a returning reader needs to *find* on the dashboard.
2. **Match the form-layer family that's already declared in the header subtitle** — *"An Appalachian Almanac for Fernwood."* Saved entries became "The Almanac" because that word was already in the genre frame the reader had been handed at the top of the page. The new card collected up under it.

For a reference shelf in an almanac, the form-layer question is: **what does an almanac call its curated references?**

Almanacs historically pair their seasonal/practical body with a back-of-book references section. The traditional almanac form-layer names for this section are *Library*, *Sources*, *References*, *Further Reading*, *Notes*. Of those, the one with strongest in-form lineage and the cleanest physical mental model is **The Library** — the bookshelf in the corner of the room the almanac was written from.

### The four candidates

| Candidate | Form-layer? | Anchor risk | Surface-as-physical-object? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **"The Library"** | Yes — almanac form-cousin | Low (the library *is* this property's, lives on this dashboard) | Yes — a bookshelf has a clear mental model | **Recommend.** |
| "References" | Yes — academic form | Could-be-anyone fails. References belong to *any* document. | No — abstract category | Reject. |
| "Sources" | Yes — newsroom/research form | Same could-be-anyone fail as References. | No | Reject. |
| "Further Reading" | Yes — magazine/book form | Lower could-be-anyone risk than References (carries a "we read these, you might too" implication), but voice-fluent without being load-bearing. Doesn't *do* anything for the dashboard the Almanac frame hasn't already done. | Sort of — implies more pages | Strong runner-up. |

### Why "The Library" and not "Further Reading"

**"Further Reading"** is what I'd recommend at a publication that hadn't already declared its genre as almanac. It's the magazine form. It's polite, in-voice, and reads as warm.

**"The Library"** wins on three counts:

1. **Anchor.** It claims a physical object on this property. Almanacs were historically written from libraries — the bookshelf the writer-naturalist returned to. A library is a *place*, like Fernwood and the Etowah and the estate. "Further Reading" is a behavior the reader is invited into; "The Library" is a thing that exists.
2. **Family with "The Almanac."** Both are *form-layer place-nouns with a definite article.* "The Almanac." "The Library." They sit next to each other on the dashboard like two named rooms. The grammar reinforces the dual-frame: voice = field journal kept in a place, form = an almanac and its library.
3. **Survives growth without needing acknowledgment.** A library is implicitly a *growing collection.* "Further Reading" reads as a finite list of next-steps; the surface Paul described is more than that — it's the running shelf of sources the dashboard's claims rest on. "Library" carries that without saying so. (Answers question 5 below.)

### Trade-offs

- **"The Library" could read precious if the rest of the dashboard wasn't already in voice.** It isn't — Fernwood, The Almanac, the white pines on the property, the Sand County register all earn this register. One more named room is fine.
- **Risk of forcing future card names into "The X" family.** Not actually a risk: Plants, Wildlife, Vehicles, Weather are utilitarian on purpose because they're *kind* names, not *named* rooms. "The Almanac" and "The Library" earned the article because they're things, not categories. New surfaces will sort themselves.
- **One real warning sign:** if Paul, looking at the card name in the strip, ever feels the urge to call it "the references card" in conversation — that means "The Library" didn't take, and we're back to "References" or "Further Reading." That's the test at T+30.

### Anti-recommendation

I considered "**The Shelf**" briefly — physical, anchored, almanac-coded, even slightly cozier than "The Library." Rejected: too cute, drifts toward the field-journal-fluent-but-not-anchored trap "The Place Itself" fell into. "The Library" is more honest about what it is.

## F2 — Subtitle — recommend "What we read to learn this place"

**Severity:** important

**Voice principle invoked:** Almanac-rename precedent — *anchored, voice-fluent, short.* "Notes on the estate" landed because it kept the field-journal register in *"Notes on"* while anchoring concretely in place (*"the estate"*).

### Three drafts, with my recommendation up front

**Draft A (recommend):** *"What we read to learn this place"*
- *we* = shared stewardship (Paul + Mom + brother). Charter principle from 2026-05-20 — *we* is for shared stewardship of the place, not warmth shorthand. Fits.
- *learn this place* = anchored. Not "learn about the land" or "learn about gardening." This place. Singular, definite. Mirrors the could-be-anyone test directly into the subtitle.
- Verb tense (*"read"*) carries past-and-ongoing — implicit running-list (answers question 5).

**Draft B:** *"The shelf behind the dashboard"*
- Physical extension of "The Library" title. "Behind the dashboard" is a clean metaphor — the references that *support* the claims surfacing in the cards above.
- Risk: more clever than honest. Reads more like a tagline than a subtitle. Fernwood doesn't do taglines elsewhere.

**Draft C:** *"Where the dashboard's claims come from"*
- Most honest. Names the actual job of this card: it's the audit trail for every fact the rest of Fernwood asserts.
- Risk: register slip toward the operator/journalistic register. *"Claims"* is a research-pass word, not a Sand County word. Mom won't bounce off it, but it sits differently than "Notes on the estate."

### Why A wins

It does what "Notes on the estate" did — keeps the field-journal voice (*"What we read"*) while anchoring concretely in place (*"this place"*). Same shape, same calibration.

It also passes the Leopold test: *"What we read to learn this place"* would not look out of place on a Leopold page. *"The shelf behind the dashboard"* would; Leopold doesn't talk about dashboards.

### Expect Paul to override

The Almanac precedent was: Paul accepted the title and overrode the subtitle to anchor more concretely. Three places he might push:

- *"this place"* might want to be *"Fernwood"* or *"the estate"* — more anchored to a real name. *Draft A-alt:* *"What we read to learn Fernwood."*
- He might want the subtitle to carry the *currency* of the shelf (it's growing) — *Draft D:* *"What we keep reading, and what we've read so far."* (Heavier, but explicit.)
- He might think the whole subtitle is unnecessary given the card title is already form-layer and self-explanatory. Empty subtitle is a legitimate option for The Library if he wants the card to be confident.

## F3 — Category-section labels — keep utilitarian, but rewrite the eight to be utilitarian-plain

**Severity:** important

**Voice principle invoked:** *Could-be-anyone* test against the form (an almanac library section is a place where labels can be plain — sometimes plain *is* the form).

### The honest read on this question

This is the question where the right answer is the slightly boring one. Coded section labels ("Plant lore," "Wildlife counts," "The land beneath") are the trap. They'd feel:

- **Coy** — the exact word Paul flagged as a risk. *"Plant lore"* over a section that's mostly UGA Extension PDFs and NWF databases reads as costumed.
- **Inaccurate** — the contents under those headers are *organizations and publications and APIs*. They're not "lore"; they're institutional sources. Naming them lore betrays the work that went into curating them.
- **Inconsistent with the rest of the dashboard's labels** — Plants, Wildlife, Weather, Vehicles, Property, The Almanac, The Library. The card titles do the work of being in form/voice. The section labels *inside* a card don't need to also be in voice; they need to help the reader find what they're looking for. Same posture as how Plants > Care types are called "prune / fertilize / propagate" not "the season's work."

### But — the eight existing labels do need a light pass

Current labels are *utilitarian-clinical* (research-pass register, not reader register). Rewrite to *utilitarian-plain* (what a reader sees in a clean almanac):

| Current (clinical) | Recommended (plain) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Category 1: Extension & academia (Georgia-specific) | **Extension and academia** | Drop "Category N:" numbering; drop the parenthetical scope note (it's the *whole library's* scope, doesn't need restating). |
| Category 2: Native plants & habitat | **Native plants and habitat** | Same; *and* over `&` for the reader-facing register. |
| Category 3: Wildlife (state & federal) | **Wildlife — state and federal** | Em-dash reads more reference-shelf, less spreadsheet. |
| Category 4: Land, soil, water | **Land, soil, water** | Already plain; drop the "Category 4:" prefix. |
| Category 5: Fishing & aquatic (Lake Sequoyah / regional) | **Fishing and the lake** | Anchor to *the lake* (Lake Sequoyah is the lake) — passes could-be-anyone test in a way "Fishing & aquatic" doesn't. |
| Category 6: Climate, dark sky, homesteading-adjacent | **Climate, dark sky, homesteading** | Drop "-adjacent" (research-pass hedge); fine without it. |
| Category 7: History & cultural heritage (local & regional) | **History and cultural heritage** | Drop the parenthetical — the whole library is local/regional by definition. |
| Category 8: Local events & day trips (within ~45 min) | **Events and day trips nearby** | "Nearby" carries the "within ~45 min" claim implicitly and reads more reader-side. |

**What I'm explicitly *not* doing:** I'm not promoting the labels to almanac-coded poetic versions. *Wildlife — state and federal* is the right register for a library shelf. *Counts of the wild* would be wrong. The card title and subtitle carry the voice; the section headers carry navigation.

### The risk of going almanac-coded anyway

If Paul wants to push toward more almanac-coded section labels, the test for each candidate is whether a stranger reading just the label could correctly guess what was under it. *"Plant lore"* fails for the UGA-Extension-heavy section. *"The land beneath"* could plausibly work for Land/Soil/Water (it's literal and observational). But going coded on some sections and not others reads worse than going plain on all eight.

## F4 — Per-entry framing — keep Paul's prose; patch only the first sentence where it slips

**Severity:** important; bounded scope (not 85 rewrites)

**Voice principle invoked:** *Anchor the first sentence; let the rest be encyclopedic if it must* (Fernwood charter). Same pattern that fixed the plant guides.

### The sample I read

I sampled across categories — Cat 1 (UGA Extension B 1339, UGA Pickens County office), Cat 2 (Audubon Plants for Birds, Mt. Cuba Center), Cat 6 (NCEI Climate Data, PRISM, DarkSky International), Cat 7 (New Georgia Encyclopedia entries). Pattern:

**The "What it is" lines are clean, neutral, encyclopedic.** They describe what the source IS. These are fine — they're the reference-card equivalent of a card catalog entry, and they should read like that.

**The "Why it's relevant here" lines are where Paul's voice is.** Most are already anchored to *this property* — *"The single most geographically-targeted Extension publication for the property,"* *"The property's 2,959 ft elevation puts it in the kind of high-elevation refuge…"*, *"At 2,959 ft on a Blue Ridge ridge, the closest NWS station doesn't represent your microclimate."* These already pass the could-be-anyone test. They're the voice-fluent layer of each entry, and they're already doing the work.

**The "Dashboard integration idea" lines are operator-mode notes.** These are *Paul talking to himself about how to use the source.* Mom doesn't need to read them. Question for Paul: **should "Dashboard integration idea" lines surface in The Library card at all, or are they hidden in the markdown for Paul's own working purposes?** My recommendation: hide them. They're working-doc, not reader-doc. Their presence in the card would break voice (they're imperative project-management prose: *"link from any Plants > By Species detail card,"* *"Property profile subtitle:"*) and they're not load-bearing for a reader. (Adding to open questions.)

**The "Depth tier" lines are catalog metadata.** Fine to surface as a small tag/chip on each entry (*"Surface fact," "Deep-dive link," "Live data source"*). Not prose.

### Posture recommendation

**Default: keep all "What it is" and "Why it's relevant here" prose as-is.** This is Paul's voice in his working register, and it's good. The could-be-anyone test passes on almost every "Why it's relevant" line because Paul has been anchoring those to *this property* across the whole research pass.

**Exception — the marketing slip-throughs.** Sample turned up a handful of entries where the "Why it's relevant" line reads as ad-copy rather than property-anchored observation. Examples from the sample:

- DarkSky International — *"Property at Bortle 3 — exceptional for the Eastern US."* — *exceptional* is the marketing-adjective slip. Pass would be *"Property at Bortle 3 — a top tier this far east."*
- Mt. Cuba — *"directly applicable to the Hydrangea entries"* — already fine, no patch needed.
- Most entries — already in voice. Don't touch.

**Light pass, not a rewrite.** When the card surface is built, read through and flag entries where the first sentence of "Why it's relevant" leans on marketing adjectives or could-be-anyone constructions. Estimate: 5-10 entries out of 85, not 85.

### What I'm *not* recommending

- ❌ Rewrite all 85 entries in field-journal voice. That would take days and replace working-doc clarity with voice-fluency-without-anchoring. The current prose is *more* anchored than a field-journal rewrite would be.
- ❌ Soften the "What it is" lines toward field-journal voice. These are encyclopedic by design and a reference shelf rewards encyclopedic precision in the "what it is" slot. Don't touch.
- ❌ Add a per-entry "field note" prose layer that re-narrates each source. Over-engineered, would make 85 entries read as 85 mini-essays.

## F5 — "Running list" aspect — implicit, no words, no timestamps

**Severity:** nice-to-have (defer to evidence)

**Voice principle invoked:** *Defer affordances pending signal* (cross-cutting). The library *is* implicitly growing — that's what a library does. Acknowledging it in copy would be product-management prose creeping into voice.

### My read

Paul flagged this as something to *consider*, not something he's decided about. My honest recommendation is: **don't acknowledge it in the v1.** Three reasons:

1. **"The Library" carries it.** Libraries grow. That's the form. A footer line saying *"Updated as new sources are added"* would announce a thing the form already implies.
2. **"First added" timestamps on every entry would clutter the card.** 85 entries × dates = visual noise. The Almanac card carries timestamps because timestamps are part of the form there (a dated entry); a library entry doesn't have a date in its form. Date doesn't help a reader use a reference.
3. **Same pattern as defer-affordances-pending-signal:** if Paul or Mom ever comes back to the card wanting to know *"what's new here?"*, then build a "Recently added" surface. Until then, no signal, no affordance.

### What I'd do instead

Nothing visible. Internally, if Paul wants to track adds over time for his own curation, an optional `addedDate` field per entry in the markdown (Paul-only, not rendered) is fine — same pattern as the photos `qualityPhotosForFutureIntegration` tracker. But that's data hygiene, not reader-facing copy.

### When to revisit

If Paul ever finds himself in the card thinking *"wait, when did I add the Mt. Cuba reports?"* — that's the signal. Until then, the library is the library.

## Principles to propose to the library

### Candidate (Fernwood-specific, for the charter)

**Principle:** *Form-layer naming carries growth implicitly.*
**Statement:** When a UI surface holds a growing collection (saved entries, a reference shelf, observations), name the surface after the *form* (almanac, library, journal) and let the form's lineage carry the "this grows over time" truth. Don't add copy that announces growth ("updated regularly," "added as we find new ones") — almanac-form names already imply it.
**Why:** From the 2026-05-21 reference-card review. The "running list" question came up; the right answer was the form-name itself carried the implication. Naming "The Library" already says "this is a growing shelf"; saying so in copy would be product-management prose creeping into voice. Pairs with the existing form-anchor / voice-anchor principle and with defer-affordances-pending-signal.
**When it applies:** Any UI surface holding a growing collection. Card-title decisions and surface-naming generally.
**Avoid:** Footer lines that announce currency (*"Updated as new sources are added"*); "Last updated" timestamps in card chrome that exist purely to claim freshness; explicit "running list" language in subtitle copy.
**Example:**
- Fail: A subtitle reading *"A growing list — updated as we find new sources."*
- Pass: A subtitle reading *"What we read to learn this place"* under a card titled *"The Library"* — the form carries the rest.

(Hold as Fernwood-specific until a second project's surface produces the same pattern, then consider promoting.)

### Candidate (cross-project, on second confirming evidence)

The "anchored naming beats field-journal-fluent naming" principle on the Fernwood charter just earned its second piece of evidence (Property card → "Fernwood"; reference card → "The Library" over "References / Further Reading"). The pattern is repeating *within* Fernwood. That's not yet a second-*project* confirmation, but the case for promoting that principle to cross-project is getting stronger. Worth a flag: the next Bolo Boys naming decision is the natural test.
