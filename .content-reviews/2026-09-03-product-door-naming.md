## 1 · The brief, restated

Paul has ruled **one door for everyone**: a single custom domain that Mom, Paul and his brother all
open, behind which the estate — Fernwood now, the Atlanta condo later, Bob's household later still — is
chosen **by grant, never by the address**. So the domain names the **product**, and the product has no
name. His instinct is the surname (*Kirschenbauer Estates / Estates / portfolio*); his constraints are
that it feel **warm**, **not over-reference AI** ("that's not really the main selling point"), carry
the *personalized journey and guide* rather than one place's genre — *"it's not just Fernwood, it's
also the condo, it's also the vehicles"* — and be **flexible to other properties and users**, which he
flags as getting ahead of himself. Against that sit three canon constraints: `estate` **never reaches a
user-facing surface**; *"Almanac"* may never become the portable noun, because a genre promise a
gardenless condo cannot keep is a confidently-wrong record in the one string that cannot be hedged;
and any *`<noun>` manager / hub / portal / dashboard / OS* is dead because it **names the reader as an
operator of their own home.**

### 2c · Arguing "Kirschenbauer Estates" past its two frictions, in writing, as asked

It gets further than expected, then dies.

- ✅ **Past the `estate` rule — partly, and legitimately.** The ratified reason `estate` never reaches a
  user is that it breaks as a **class noun**: *"your estates," "add an estate"* make a reader meet the
  category her thing belongs to, and *she is not at an estate, she is at Fernwood.* **"Kirschenbauer
  Estates" is not a class noun** — it is a proper noun naming one family's holdings, the way *"the
  Vanderbilt houses"* does, and never asks her to learn a taxonomy. The rule does not strictly forbid
  it; saying otherwise would be citing a rule past its own reason.
- ⛔ **Not past the death sense — the plural makes it worse.** *"The Kirschenbauer Estate"* is the
  phrase on probate paperwork and the plural is one letter away. In a product holding warranties and
  contractor agreements, whose owner at Fernwood is Paul's mother, that is low-probability, non-zero,
  and free to avoid — free on a selector header, where the rule was written, **not** free on the
  permanent address of the whole product.
- ⛔ **Not past the development.** In Pickens County it parses as a **subdivision** next to Tate
  Mountain Estates — the confusion `CLAUDE.md` spends a paragraph preventing.
- ⛔ **So the decisive reason is the probate register, not the spelling** (§2b.1 retires the dictation
  argument I had in this slot). **The neighbour it finds is the bare surname (B)** — same warmth, no
  class noun, no institutional noun, and the researcher's read points here: *"surname + a warm
  place-word ≠ surname + an institutional noun; only the second reads as a form."* B dies at Bob.

### 2d · What "portfolio" and "estates" cost in this family's mouths

- **"Portfolio" is dead twice.** **Triple-booked in Paul's own vocabulary** — securities (a private
  financial dashboard, a thesis substrate) and his career portfolio — and it fails the ratified
  principle directly: **a portfolio is a thing you administer for a return**, `<noun> manager` in a
  finance accent, naming Mom as operator of the house she lives in. ⭐ It is also this project's
  twice-named **anti-persona** (*"the portfolio property manager"*, researcher §3).
- **"Estates" reads as property management** — a letting agency's register, and the one word here that
  makes the record sound like it exists for accounting rather than for noticing.

## 4 · The one I would pick: **Porchlight** — `porchlight.place` (`.com` if the check surprises us)

Three reasons, in order of weight. **It is the only candidate passing all nine checkable rows.**

1. **It survives every instance that is actually planned** (row 9). Fernwood has a porch; the condo has
   a light by a door; Bob's house is his own and the name makes no claim on it; the fleet fits — you
   leave the light on for whoever is still coming back. **No other candidate clears all four.**
2. **It is warm without being a function** (rows 3–7). A porch light is something you leave on **for**
   someone — the precise inverse of *manager / hub / portal / dashboard / OS / portfolio*. It lands the
   warmth Paul asked for nowhere near AI, seasons, property or accounting, and promises **welcome**,
   the one promise every instance can keep.
3. **It says nothing anyone has to learn** (rows 1–2). Ten characters, one pronunciation,
   self-explaining. For a reader whose fear is getting things wrong, a name with nothing to get wrong
   beats a name with a better story.

### 4a · ⚠️ This revises my own 09-02 position, and the revision is the interesting part

On 09-02 I held the product should be called **nothing**, with the falsifier *"the first time someone
who is not Paul, Mom or Bob has to say the name out loud, it needs one."* That has still not fired —
**but the premise moved, as `VOCABULARY.md` §3b already records**, and Paul has now ruled that all
three meet a door. So the answer changes from *no name* to *a name that does not compete.* That inverts
the could-be-anyone test at this layer: **a shell name may be warm-generic precisely because the rooms
are not.** Fernwood's `<h1>` stays *Fernwood*, its authored subtitle stays *"An Appalachian Almanac for
282 Church Mountain Road"* — every anchored word stays where it is, and a door as anchored as the rooms
would be a brand competing with the thing she came for. **Porchlight fails could-be-anyone as a room
name and passes as a door name**, and that distinction is the finding.

### 4b · Falsifier — what would show this was wrong within a month of her using it

**`user-researcher` §5 owns the cutover falsifier** (≥3 sessions on ≥2 active days in 7 days, plus the
zero-sessions ambiguity trap). Mine falsifies **the name rather than the migration**:

1. ⛔ **She reads a promise into it that Fernwood then breaks** — anything like *"is Porchlight the one
   with the plants?"* means the shell has started competing with the room and §4a's claim fails. Low
   expected probability, and the one thing I would not otherwise learn.
2. ⛔ **She says it back wrong, or won't say it** (row 10). Falsifies **D specifically**; remedy is C.
3. ⚠️ **She already has a word for it** (row 11) — every candidate loses to hers, and *adopt their
   words, never improve them* decides it without me. **The asymmetry that makes this cheap:** a wrong
   name costs one more cutover, while a **surname** domain cannot be un-published (§5.1).

## 5 · What I did not decide — Paul's

1. ⭐ **The surname in a public domain — sharper than "discoverable."** `viewer.html:6` sets
   `noindex, nofollow`, so the page is not in search results; **a domain is not protected by that.**
   Pages enforces HTTPS, and every HTTPS hostname is written permanently into public **Certificate
   Transparency logs** — third-party, searchable, outside Paul's control; WHOIS privacy hides the
   registrant, not the name. Measured: the live page already renders **`credit: "Paul Kirschenbauer"`**
   and the street address **14 times**, so a surname domain publishes a **name → address** link,
   permanently, for a product whose next instance is his mother's home — an address kept in `.private/`
   *precisely because it is sensitive.* **The identity-record spine's call, not Fernwood's** (researcher
   §4 independently). D and E are surname-free; C keeps the family and drops the index.
2. **Branded product, or a thing he builds for people he knows.** Still the only question that changes
   the answer, still open in `PRODUCT-ENGINE.md` Q6. Everything above is stable under either answer
   *except* the weight on §2a.
3. **The cutover itself** — whether Mom's saved path is migrated, when, and by whom. Not a copy call,
   and the researcher's §5 says it is the biggest risk in the move.
4. **Her icon label, a separate string from the domain** — researcher §5 recommends leaving it
   *Fernwood* at one grant, upstream of her grant count. **I agree; not my call.** ⭐ And note what
   follows: if her label never changes, **the name is for Paul, the brother and Bob.**
5. **Whether C's short form is a thing this family says**, and **what she already calls it** (rows
   10–11). Either outranks every candidate here.
6. **`.place` vs `.house` vs `.com`** — pending a real availability check, which no model read replaces.
