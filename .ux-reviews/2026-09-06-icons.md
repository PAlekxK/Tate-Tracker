# The emoji audit — are these the right icons, and is there a better set?

**Review date:** 2026-09-06 · **Reviewer:** ux-expert · **Mode:** review + options (system level)
**Subject:** every glyph doing ICON work in `engine/viewer.template.html`, `onboarding/index.html`,
`estate/index.html`, `homes/index.html`, `settings/place/index.html`, `settings/account/index.html`

⛔ **Nothing was edited.** No page, no template, no CSS. This is a specification and a decision brief.

**Paul's ask, verbatim:** *"All the emojis that are being used — now is a good time to really
double-check, now that we've refined how we look at everything and our overall tone: are these emojis
right? Is there another kind of icon library we can use — a little more modern and clean and
personalised?"*

## 0. User context

- **Primary user:** the half-engaged reader at 414 × 848, text mode **A+**, one-handed, often older,

- **Principles applied:** `Icons earn their place — true AND useful` + its button-glyph sharpening

  `Make every surface read at half-engagement` (fernwood) · `One shell, one signal each` (fernwood,
  candidate) · tonight's colour ladder (`.ux-reviews/2026-09-06-colour-scheme.md`).

## 2. What emoji-as-icons actually costs here

| Cost | Bites Fernwood? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Colour is baked into the platform font — it cannot take the scheme** | ⭐ **Yes, hardest.** Tonight's ladder derives 11 tokens from one hex. Every emoji ignores all of them. A Dusk household gets a purple masthead, purple buttons, purple links — and a green 🌿, a red 🚩, a blue 🛻. | `.ux-reviews/2026-09-06-colour-scheme.md` §1 |
| **Renders differently per platform** | Yes, and it is about to matter. Mom is iPhone; the second household is not guaranteed to be. Apple/Google/Windows draw the same codepoint as different objects, and ~25% of users don't know it happens. | [ACM CSCW 2018](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3274393), [emodji.com](https://www.emodji.com/articles/cross-platform-emoji-differences/) |
| **Screen readers announce the Unicode NAME, not the intent** | **Yes, and partly unguarded.** `.js-glyph` and `.ic-head-icon` correctly carry `aria-hidden="true"`. **The 17 `.main-card-icon` squares do not** — VoiceOver reads *"pickup truck, Vehicles"*. `.pmap-btn-emoji` doesn't either: *"wastebasket, Delete this place."* | [Pope Tech](https://blog.pope.tech/2026/04/01/making-emojis-and-icons-screen-reader-accessible/), [Tink](https://tink.uk/accessible-emoji/) |
| **Size & optical alignment at A+** | Yes. Emoji carry their own baseline and ink extent, so 📓 and 🏔️ sit differently inside the same 42px square, and at the jump strip's 19px several degrade to coloured specks — the exact failure `Icons earn their place` names. | `:4214`, `:4220` |
| **Register** | Mixed — see §4 F4. | — |
| **Where emoji is the RIGHT choice** | ⭐ **Depiction.** 🌑🌒🌓 *is* the moon's shape. ☀️⛅🌧️ *is* the sky. 🐦 on a bird record is a picture of the subject. A monoline pictogram is strictly worse at all three: it abstracts away the one thing the reader wanted. Also right for **canon-authored identity** (`emoji` per species/vehicle) — a household picks from a keyboard it already owns. | `fernwood.md` "Prefer" clause |

## 3. The options

**Constraint first: no fetch, ever.** Onboarding's rule — *loading a font contacts a third party
before the reader has done anything* — plus the site's physical premise (no cell, Wi-Fi from the
house) rules out **all** icon-font CDNs, including Material Symbols' variable font. **Inline only.**
That is a filter, not a preference, and it kills the easiest integration path for every library below.

| | **(a) Keep emoji, curated** | **(b) Inline SVG sprite** | **(c) Hybrid — chrome converts, subject stays emoji** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Takes the scheme colour | ❌ never | ✅ `stroke="currentColor"` inherits the token ladder for free | ✅ where it matters |
| Cross-platform identical | ❌ | ✅ | ✅ on chrome |
| Screen reader | needs `aria-hidden` on 17+ nodes | ✅ `aria-hidden` on one `<use>` pattern | ✅ |
| Legible at 19px | ⚠️ several don't | ✅ 24px grid, 2px stroke, designed for it | ✅ |
| Register risk | none (it's what ships) | ⚠️ **monoline stroke = the SaaS-admin drawing language `fernwood.md` warns about** | ⚠️ two icon languages on one card strip |
| Bytes | 0 | ~40 icons ≈ **+16 KB**, +`<use>` sites ≈ **+20 KB total (~2%)** | ≈ **+8 KB (~0.8%)**, ~20 icons |
| Effort | low | high | **medium** |

**The libraries, if we inline one** (all permissive; none requires runtime attribution):

| Set | Licence | Icons | Style | Fit here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lucide** ⭐ | **ISC** (139 Feather-derived icons stay MIT) | ~1,600 | 24px grid, **1.5–2px stroke, rounded caps** | ⭐ **Recommended.** The rounded cap is the least clinical of the monoline sets — it reads warm rather than enterprise. ~540 B raw per icon; ~350 B inside a `<symbol>`. |
| **Phosphor** | MIT | ~9,000 | **six weights** — thin/light/regular/bold/fill/duotone | The only set whose *weight* is a personalisation axis (A+ could step to Bold). Widest coverage. Heavier to curate. |
| **Tabler** | MIT | ~5,900 | 24px, 2px stroke, rounded | Near-identical to Lucide, larger and slightly more technical in feel. |
| **Heroicons** | MIT | 316 ×4 styles | outline 24 / solid 24 / **mini 20 / micro 16** | The *micro/mini* optical sizes are genuinely useful at the jump strip's 19px. Coverage is thin — 316 will not cover a field journal. |
| **Material Symbols** | Apache 2.0 | ~3,700 | variable font (weight/fill/grade/optical size) | ⛔ The variable font is a **fetch** — disqualified in its intended form. Static SVGs are fine but throw away the whole point of the set. |
| ⛔ **SF Symbols** | Apple licence | — | — | **Cannot ship.** Apple's terms forbid use outside Apple platforms. Worth saying, since "Apple app" is tonight's register word. |

## 5. Recommendation — hybrid, Lucide, chrome only

**Convert the chrome. Keep the wildlife.** The split is not a compromise, it is the honest reading of
what each glyph is doing: a 🗑 is a *control* and wants to be a pictogram that takes her colour; a 🌒
is a *depiction* and a pictogram would destroy it.

**Converts (~20 icons, ≈8 KB inlined):** the 6 jump-strip glyphs · 💬 · ＋ · ✎ 🚩 🗑 ✕ · ▶ ❚❚ · ⬇ ⬆ ·
📡 🌐 📊 · the two composer SVGs (already SVG) · 🆕.
**Does NOT convert:** the 16 card identity squares · `WEATHER_CODES` · moon phases · the care lexicon
(ratified) · celestial · species emoji from canon · **✓** (ratified affirmative mark, and a tick is
already a shape not a picture).

**Mechanics.** One `<symbol>` sprite in a `hidden` `<svg>` at the top of `<body>`; every use site is
`<svg class="i" aria-hidden="true"><use href="#i-truck"/></svg>`; `fill:none; stroke:currentColor` so
the icon inherits `--accent` / `--accent-ink` / `--ink` from whatever context it sits in — **which is
how the scheme reaches the icons for free.** No fetch, no font, no build step. Byte cost ≈ **+20 KB
on a ~1.05 MB file (~2%)**; the 1 MB cliff is already crossed and mitigated by the Blob fallback, so
the real discipline is *subset only* — **never inline a whole library** (Lucide entire ≈ 500 KB).

**Migration order — ten moves, cheapest-risk first.** Do **F2 (aria-hidden)** and **F4 (the 🟢 and the
ops glyphs)** before any of this; they are free and they do not depend on the decision.

| # | Move | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **💬 → speech-bubble icon**, 5 files | It sits **on** the accent gradient (`:4954`). The one glyph that visibly fights the new scheme, and it is on every surface. |
| 2–7 | **The six jump-strip glyphs** ⛅🛻🧰🏠🌿🐾 | Smallest rendered size (17/19px) and the **only affordance with a 100% measured tap rate**. Biggest legibility win per byte. |
| 8 | **＋** (`homes:112`, ask-next `:17662`) | A fullwidth typographic plus is not even an emoji — it renders inconsistently and has no fallback. |
| 9 | **✎ 🚩 🗑 ✕** (zone panel) | Pure control chrome; 🗑 is the app's one destructive control and currently announces as *"wastebasket."* |
| 10 | **📡 🌐 📊** (provenance chips) | Trust surface, rendered ~11px inside a coloured oval — colour-speck territory. **Keep the three-way distinction**, and keep them off the accent ladder (colour-review §2). |

## 6. "Personalised" — what it can and cannot mean

| ✅ It can mean | ❌ It should not mean |
|---|---|
| **The scheme tints them.** `currentColor` + tonight's ladder = every chrome icon is her colour, on every surface, from one hex. This is the real answer to Paul's word, and it is **impossible with emoji.** | **A household picking an icon *set*.** A style switcher doubles the sprite, makes every future icon a matrix decision, and buys the reader nothing she can name. |
| **Weight follows the reader.** Phosphor ships six weights; Lucide takes a `stroke-width`. A+ mode could step the stroke 1.5 → 2px, which is a real legibility gain for an older reader — *the app getting bolder because she reads bigger.* | **Per-icon colour overrides.** The whole point of the ladder is one input and derived values; a per-icon override re-opens the 162-hand-mixed-colours problem the 08-24 audit measured. |
| **A household choosing its own SUBJECT glyphs** — the `emoji` field already in canon. Bob's horses, someone's boat. Keep this as **emoji**, from the keyboard she owns. | **A household naming the chrome.** The trash can is the trash can. Personalising a control is how a control stops being learnable. |

## Questions for Paul (4)

1. ⭐ **Register.** Does tonight's *"polished, professional, crisp and clear, on the line of an Apple
   app"* license a **monoline stroke set** on the chrome — given `fernwood.md`'s standing ban on
   glyphs *"at home in PagerDuty, JIRA, a SaaS admin panel"*? My read is that the rule bans the
   **semantics** (traffic lights, warning triangles, severity chips), not the **stroke**, and that
   Apple's own language is monoline. But that rule is ratified and this is the one call I will not
   make for you.
2. **Do the 16 card identity squares stay emoji?** My recommendation is **yes** — they are the
   strongest identity signal she has at A+, they are subject-depiction not chrome, and converting
   them is the single biggest change to a surface she has learned. The cost of my answer is honest:
   **the card strip would then carry two icon languages**, emoji squares above pictogram controls.
3. **Lucide or Phosphor?** Lucide (ISC, rounded caps, warmest of the monoline sets) is my pick.
   Phosphor (MIT, six weights) is the pick if you want **weight-follows-text-size** as a real
   personalisation axis rather than a nice idea.
4. **📚 twice** (Reference drawer + Sources, one nested in the other) — fix now as part of this pass,
   or is the parent/child relationship enough that it reads as deliberate?

## Principles this review would propose

1. **Depiction keeps its picture; chrome takes the scheme.** *Scope: cross-project.* An icon that
   *shows the subject* (the moon's shape, the sky, the animal) is content and should stay pictorial.
   An icon that *operates the app* is chrome and must be able to inherit the app's colour, weight and
   platform. Falsifier: *if replacing the glyph with a grey outline destroys information, it was
   depiction.* **2nd occurrence** of the depiction/chrome seam — the first was the 2026-07-07
   *"register is carried by chrome, not just words"* candidate.
2. **A personalisation token must reach every layer, or the unreachable layer becomes the leftover.**
   *Scope: cross-project.* Tonight's scheme reaches type, fills, edges and ground — and stops at the
   icons, which is precisely where the eye goes first. **2nd occurrence** of *"a personalisation
   token must default to the literal it replaces"* (colour review), from the opposite direction:
   that one is about not breaking the default, this one about not stranding a layer.
