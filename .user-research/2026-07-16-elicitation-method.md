# Getting the map out of her head — is elicitation a valid instrument, and what's the method?

**Provenance caveat, unchanged from round 1.** Her verbatim is gone. Every claim about
what she *said* is `[relayed]` — Paul's memory, one remove, self-declared incomplete.
Claims about the *record* are `[validated]`. The argument below depends on not mixing them.

---

## 1. Paul's epistemics claim — does it survive?

> "There's no way to make the map match the mental model she has because it's just in
> her head… I don't think it's trustworthy that if I do it, it'll be right compared to
> what she has in her head." `[relayed]`

**The claim survives. Completely. It is the most correct thing said about this project
in two rounds.** `[inferred, strong]` She lives the property; Paul is in Atlanta. `zoneId`
is the one field in the entire record he physically cannot supply — round 1 established
that independently `[validated]`. If he authors the map, he authors *his* map and the
record's central lie ("this is a journal of her place") persists, now laundered through
a confirmation tap. He is right to distrust his own version.

**But the claim contains a hidden step, and the whole design is built on it.**

> "Only she has the map, so only she can **draw** it."

Two different verbs are wearing one coat:

| | Who | Can it be delegated? |
|---|---|---|
| **Source** the map — hold it, know it, speak it | Only Mom | **No.** Non-delegable. Paul is right. |
| **Draw** the map — transcribe it into the record | ??? | **Yes.** This is ordinary scribe work. |

Paul's argument establishes the first row and then quietly claims the second. It doesn't
follow. Every interview ever run has this shape: the participant sources, the researcher
records. Nobody calls a transcript the researcher's invention. `[assumption]`

**So: does "only she can draw it" run into "she won't do a project"?**

Both are true, and they do not collide — they are about different things. Paul's is a
claim about **where the knowledge lives**. Round 1's was a claim about **what she will
do** (mobile-only, reads with difficulty, 0-for-3 on standing controls, obligation-averse
`[validated]`). Nothing in the reframe touches that evidence; the reframe is about
epistemics, the bet was about behavior.

**The trap is what happens when you accept both AND insist she operates the instrument.**
Then: the only person who can supply it won't use the only surface offered to supply it,
and the tool cannot have the thing it needs. That trap is real — **and it is entirely
manufactured by the hidden step.** Drop "only she can draw it," keep "only she can source
it," and it dissolves.

**The residue that is not manufactured** `[relayed]`: *"I don't wanna do it."* Being the
scribe for twenty minutes is **not** the same as inventing the map — it is the precise
opposite of it, and it's the only move that honors Paul's own epistemics. Inventing the
map is the thing he correctly refuses. Writing down what she says is how he avoids
inventing it. If "I don't want to do it" extends to *transcribing a phone call with his
mother*, then the map has no channel out of her head at all, and the honest answer is:
**the record never gets her map; it gets filled one card at a time over months by Paul
asking.** That's a viable outcome (round 1's F3 predicted it) — but it should be chosen
knowingly, not arrived at by building a surface she won't touch.

## 2. The anchoring horn — resolved, not split

The brief frames it as: anchoring corrupts her map **vs.** open-ended text is
unusable for her. Both true. Both horns real.

**They are only in conflict inside the app.** The contradiction is an artifact of assuming
one medium. Unpack it:

- **Anchoring is only harmful when you are eliciting the STRUCTURE** — the vocabulary,
  the partition, the hierarchy. Show her 8 zones and you will get 8 zones back.
- **Anchoring is harmless — actively *correct*** — when you are eliciting **PLACEMENT
  into a structure she has already sourced.** "Which of *your* places is the panicle
  hydrangea in?" is not a leading question. It's a tap.
- **Her reading constraint applies to TEXT, not to TALK.** `[validated]` She reads with
  difficulty (22 A/A+ events, the only device ever to touch it). Nothing in two months of
  evidence suggests she has any difficulty *speaking*. "Open-ended is the worst possible
  instrument for her" is true of **open-ended text on a phone**. In speech, open-ended is
  the *cheapest* thing she can do — it costs her less than tapping.

**The resolution:**

> **Put the un-anchorable step in the medium where open-endedness is free (speech,
> off-app, Paul scribes). Put the anchor-tolerant step in the medium where structure is
> free (the app, one card, one tap).**

Neither horn survives, because each was only sharp in the medium the other one owns.

**Step 1 (zones) goes OUTSIDE the app. Yes — emphatically.** `[inferred, strong]` Not as a
concession, and not because a human conversation is *nicer*. Because:
1. It's the only medium where she can be open-ended at zero cost to her.
2. It's the only medium where the interviewer can follow a thread he didn't predict —
   which is the entire point, since the premise is that her map is unknown to us.
3. Round 1's precondition (capture is broken and lying) **does not apply to a phone
   call.** Every in-app instrument currently drops her input and renders "Noted — it's in
   the record. ✓" `[validated]`. A phone call cannot silently no-op.
4. It's the only medium in which Paul's distrust of his own map is *resolved* rather
   than *hidden* — he hears it from her.

**The app carries steps 2+ only.** One card. One tap. Placement, photo, confirm.

## 3. The method — story-based, not taxonomic

Do **not** ask "what are your zones?" or "what do you call the parts of the property?"
Both are taxonomy questions. Taxonomy questions produce taxonomy answers — invented on
the spot to satisfy the asker, which is a different artifact from the map she actually
uses. This is the Mom Test failure mode `[Fitzpatrick]` and it's also anchoring by
another route: the *question form* imposes a partition even when the *content* doesn't.

**Instead — story-based interviewing** (Torres; this agent's standing default posture).
The framework in one line, since Paul hasn't run one: *ask for a specific past episode in
concrete detail and let the structure fall out of the narrative, because people organize
stories using the units they actually think in, without being able to tell you what those
units are.*

### The instrument (~20 min, phone, recorded with her OK, Paul scribes)

**Phase 1 — the walk (10 min). Zero mention of zones, plants, or the app.**
- "Tell me about the last time you went out on the property — not for anything in
  particular. Sunday, say. Where'd you go first?"
- "Then where?" · "What made you go over there?" · "What'd you look at?"
- Pure narrative. He follows her feet. He does not offer a single noun.

**Phase 2 — mirror back her nouns (5 min).**
- Take **only the words she used** and read them back: "You said the [X]. What counts as
  the [X] — where's it start and stop?"
- "Is there anywhere on the place we didn't get to?"
- This is where the map gets drawn, in her vocabulary, and it is anchor-free because
  every noun in it came out of her mouth first.

**Phase 3 — LAST, and only last: the reveal (5 min).**
- *Now* show her the 8 zones. "I sketched these one night. What'd I get wrong?"
- This is anchoring — deliberately, and safely, because her map is already captured.
  Its only job is to reconcile *his* sketch against *hers*, which is a fair use of an
  anchor and the one Paul actually needs.
- **Order is load-bearing.** Phase 3 before Phase 1 destroys the instrument. If the 8
  zones enter her ear before her story leaves her mouth, the whole call produces Paul's
  map with her voice on it — the exact thing he says he doesn't trust.

**What Paul does with it:** writes down her nouns. That's the deliverable. Not a
schema, not a decision — a list of the places she named and roughly what each contains.
That is the map, and it took twenty minutes, and he did not invent one word of it.

## 4. The zone names — why load-bearing, and the question to ask

**Facts** `[validated, per brief]`: all 8 created 2026-05-28 in a 5-minute sitting,
`createdBy: device`, **6 of 8 still `status: draft`**, vertices are fractional coords on a
2015 leaf-off screenshot. Names: Fairway · Fairway Fringe · Western Garden · Eastern
Garden · Pond Area · Lower 40 · Stable Grounds · Parking Bank.

**Why it decides the design, not the copy:**

| If the names are… | Then the 8 zones are… | And the design is… |
|---|---|---|
| **Family vernacular** — words the family says out loud | **her map, already** — Paul was transcribing from memory, not inventing | **The interview mostly collapses.** Phase 3 becomes the whole call, ~5 min. Ship placement cards using her own words. Anchoring risk ≈ 0 — you cannot anchor someone to their own vocabulary. |
| **Paul's coinages** — typed to fill a shape | **a foreign taxonomy** | **Full Phase 1–2 required, and the 8 names must not reach her ears before Phase 2 ends.** Confirming them would launder Paul's map into "hers" — and produce exactly the artifact he says he doesn't trust, wearing a validation tag. |
| **Mixed** (my bet) | partly each | **Per-name handling.** Vernacular names ship as-is; coinages get elicited fresh. |

**My read** `[assumption]`: mixed, leaning coined. "Fairway" and "Lower 40" have the cadence
of inherited land vernacular — Lower 40 especially is a stock rural idiom nobody invents
under a schema deadline. "Fairway Fringe" and "Parking Bank" read as descriptive coinages
made to partition a screenshot. And **6-of-8 left `draft` in one 11pm sitting is itself a
tell** `[inferred]`: the two he marked `confirmed` are `fairway` and `pond-area-3` — the
two most likely to be real names. He may have already flagged his own uncertainty.

**The question Paul should answer — per name, not per set** (and it needs no Mom, no
build, ~5 minutes):

> **For each of the 8: have you ever heard Mom, or anyone in the family, say that name
> out loud in a sentence — or did you type it that night to have something to call it?**
> Mark each: *heard it* / *typed it* / *not sure*.

Not "did you invent these?" — that invites a defensive whole-set answer. The per-name
split is the load-bearing data, and "not sure" is a legitimate, useful answer that Phase 3
resolves.

## 5. Sequence — zones→plants→photos assumes something untested, and the relay argues against it

**What the sequence assumes** `[assumption]`: that she holds the property as a **spatial
partition**, with zones at the top of the hierarchy and plants as contents. Container-first.

**That is a hypothesis, and it is currently the design's foundation.** Alternatives she
might actually use: plants/objects · paths and routes · seasons · views from windows ·
jobs to do · sun and shade. Each produces a different top-level unit and therefore a
different first question.

**The evidence we have points away from zones-first** `[inferred]`:

1. **Her one self-built habit is object-first.** `[validated]` Photo → this plant →
   what is it. Claude on her laptop, her own workflow, her stated "difference maker."
   Nobody built it for her. It has **no spatial step at all.** It is the single most
   reliable window into how she organizes attention, and its unit is *the plant in front
   of her*, not the place she's standing.
2. **The grammar of her own relayed ask.** `[relayed — weak, but pointed]` *"Every **plant**
   should carry a picture, a description, and **location data**."* Plant is the **subject**;
   location is an **attribute of the plant**. That is plant-first with zone-as-field —
   the *inverse* of zones→plants. Paul's proposed sequence reads the relay and then
   inverts its own grammar. Weak evidence (paraphrase, from memory) — but it is *his*
   paraphrase, and it still came out plant-first.
3. **Both of her real photo submissions are plants**, not places. `[validated]`
4. Nothing in two months of behavior shows her ever using a spatial unit for anything.

**None of this proves she doesn't think in zones.** It does mean *zones-first is the least
supported ordering on the table*, and it's the one being built. `[inferred]`

**How to test it cheaply, BEFORE building the sequence:**

**The Phase 1 call *is* the test — count the nouns.** No instrument to build, no code, one
phone call, ~10 minutes. When she narrates her Sunday walk:
- She organizes by **places** ("I went down to the ___, then over to the ___") → zones are
  real and top-level. **Build the sequence as proposed.**
- She organizes by **plants** ("I went to see if the hydrangea had opened") → she thinks
  object-first. **Invert it: plants→zones→photos.** The confirm card becomes "here's a
  plant — where is it?", which is *also* the shape her existing habit already has.
- She organizes by **jobs, seasons, or routes** → both proposed orderings are wrong and
  you found out for the price of a call.

Whatever unit she spontaneously reaches for to hold the story together **is** the top of
her hierarchy. She cannot tell you what it is if you ask directly; she cannot hide it if
you ask for a story. That is the entire reason story-based interviewing exists.

**This is the cheapest test available and it gates everything downstream.** Do not build
the sequence until the call is made. `[inferred, strong]`

## 6. Tension 2 — the interview ends. Resolved by relocation, not by exception.

The brief's worry: a structured top-down elicitation is inherently finite — a sequence
with a denominator — and Fernwood's doctrine forbids completable, task-manager-shaped
surfaces.

**The worry is correct, and the recommendation dissolves it for free.** `[inferred]`

An interview ending is not a problem — **because the interview is not in the app.** The
doctrine constrains **the surface**, not a phone call with your son. A conversation is
*supposed* to end; that's what conversations do. It has no progress bar because it has no
UI. It leaves nothing visibly unfinished in the field journal, because it never entered it.

Move the finite, denominator-bearing, completable thing **off** the surface that forbids
denominators, and:
- The app never carries a sequence. It carries one card. No denominator exists.
- **No exception needs to be earned.** Doctrine holds, unamended.
- Round 1's "the experiment damages the thing it measures" is answered: nothing is parked
  in her app that can sit unfinished, because nothing was parked.

This is a **second, independent argument arriving at the same recommendation** as §2's.
That convergence is the main reason I hold it as strongly as I do.

The one thing that *would* need an exception — a multi-step in-app elicitation wizard —
is the thing this method deletes.

**Straight:** the reframe is **the same queue pointed at better questions**, plus one
genuinely new thing that isn't in the app at all. `[inferred, strong]`

- **"Take over the main screen" → no.** `[inferred, strong]` The shipped instrument —

  produced under (a) a capture path that silently no-ops and lies `[validated]`,
  (b) confirm cards with **no photo affordance whatsoever** — not a data gap, the card
  type has no `<img>` in it `[validated]`, and (c) plants the queue was asking about that
  hold no photograph at all `[validated]`. You have not measured this instrument. You have

  the gold") on evidence weaker than the panel had.
- **What IS genuinely new:** the **off-app elicitation step**. It's new because it's not a

- **What's a verified defect, not a reset:** **photos on the confirm card.** Ship it

  question, it makes every existing card better, and until it lands you are asking a
  reading-impaired user to confirm a variety read off a photograph *while showing her no
  photograph*. Her ask #4 is a bug report, and it's correct.

## 10. ADDENDUM — Paul finalizes the zones first (update, 2026-07-16)

> "I have a pretty clear picture of how she wants the zone set up, and it's just an
> adjustment from what we have put together already." `[relayed]`

This does not overturn §2 (the trap), §3 (the method), §6, §7 or §8. It **sharpens §4
and §5** and needs the following.

### 10.1 The split is coherent — but watch where the untrustworthy part keeps moving

Zones-are-known / plants-per-zone-is-locked-in-her-head is a **legitimate split**, and if
true it's a real efficiency. `[assumption]` Two flags:

1. **The claim drifted.** Yesterday: *"there's no way to make the map match the mental
   model she has because it's just in her head"* + *"I don't think it's trustworthy that
   if I do it, it'll be right."* Today the map is the part he knows. Not a contradiction
   — the *layout* could be known while *contents* aren't. But note the shape: **the thing
   he claims not to know has moved to whichever step he doesn't want to do.** Worth him
   sitting with for thirty seconds. `[assumption]`
2. **"How she wants the zones set up" is a preference claim, not a knowledge claim.**
   Her map-in-her-head ≠ her stated wishes for the schema. If he's encoding what she
   *asked for* on 7/15 (relayed; her verbatim lost), that's a **design request**, not her
   mental model — and the reframe's whole premise was that the mental model is the target.
   Different artifacts. `[inferred]`

### 10.2 Confirm-bias — and the structural problem underneath it

The bias is real and the base rate is ugly: **her 2 recorded answers are both Yes**
`[validated]`; agreeable, 70-something, reads with difficulty, and evaluating an 8-polygon
map on a 2015 leaf-off screenshot is a *reading-heavy* task — the exact modality that
fails her `[validated]`. An ~80%-right map gets "yes, that's right."

**But bias isn't the core problem. This is:**

> **A confirm question can catch a wrong name. It structurally cannot catch a missing zone.**

Not a bias — a **logical property of the instrument**. Absence is invisible on a confirm
surface. There is no affordance whose activation means *"there's one you didn't draw."*
To report an omission she'd have to compose free text — the single thing she can't do,
and the exact thing the app silently dropped on 7/15 `[validated]`.

**Recognition ≠ recall.** A recognition test cannot reveal items absent from the probe
set. The only instrument that catches omission is **free recall** — and for her, free
recall must be **speech**.

### 10.3 Is "confirm this map" recoverable? Yes — two ways

**The fix (10 min, keeps his whole sequence):** he still finalizes. He just spends **10
minutes of Phase 1 first** — *"tell me about last Sunday, where'd you go first?"* —
**before** the map exists in her ear. Then he finalizes *against her nouns*, and the
confirm becomes **reconciliation** instead of approval.

**The cheaper degradation, if he won't:** change the question shape. Don't ask *"is this
right?"* Ask *"tell me what each of these is."* **Narration surfaces omission** — she'll
say a name that isn't on the map, or describe an extent that doesn't match. Recall
smuggled into a recognition surface. Weaker than Phase 1, far better than a binary tap.

**Never:** a yes/no confirm on the finalized map as the only gate. That produces a
`validated` tag on Paul's map.

### 10.4 The zone-names question: MORE live — it's now an input, not hygiene

If he's adjusting names *to hers*, he must already know **which are hers**. That is
exactly the per-name provenance question from §4 — so it is no longer optional research
hygiene, it is **a required input to the step he's about to take.**

> **Per name, mark one: _heard her say it_ / _typed it myself_ / _not sure_.**

**Any name marked "typed it" that survives into the finalized set is Paul's word wearing
her map's name** — and step 2 will then ask her plant questions in his vocabulary. If he's
genuinely adjusting to her model, this takes five minutes and mostly confirms he was
already right. If it's hard to answer, that difficulty *is* the finding. `[inferred]`

### 10.5 What step 1 must capture for step 2 to be valid — three conditions

If Paul's finalized zones are wrong in a way she won't correct, **every zone-by-zone plant
question inherits the error** and the elicitation is his map wearing her name. For that
not to happen:

**(a) Completeness — no missing zone.** Only catchable by free recall (Phase 1). A missing
zone fails **silently and permanently**: the walk never asks about it, its plants have
nowhere to go, and they read as *non-responses* — indistinguishable from her disengaging.
Same failure shape as the dark MacBook: absence of signal misread as absence of interest.
`[inferred, strong]`

**(b) Boundary agreement, not just name agreement.** She can say "yes, Lower 40" while
meaning a **different polygon**. The name confirms; the **extent** doesn't. Then she
assigns a plant to a zone whose extent she reads differently than the record does — the
record is wrong while every field reads "confirmed." Vertices are fractional coords on a
2015 leaf-off screenshot with **no georeference** `[validated]` — there is no ground truth
to check her against. Catch it in Phase 2 (*"where's it start and stop?"*), in her words.

**(c) Per-zone provenance tags.** Each finalized zone tagged `heard-from-her` vs.
`paul-inferred`, so **step 2's answers are never trusted above their container**: a plant
placed into a `paul-inferred` zone is at most `inferred`, never `validated`. This is
Fernwood's own **provenance-honesty** principle applied to zones — and it's the
`status: draft` field the zones **already have** (6 of 8 still draft `[validated]`). Use
the field that's already there.

**The failure this prevents is evidence-laundering:** one tap promoting eight assumptions
to `validated`, with every downstream plant answer inheriting the tag. Round 1 caught the
identical shape (the ownership thesis over-read from a relayed paraphrase).

### 10.6 The trap — the update sharpens it, and hands Paul the answer

The reframe's stated reason was **distrust of his own map**. Step 1 is him drawing the
map. That's the tension — but it resolves in his favor, cheaply:

> **Finalizing 8 polygons is MORE work than a 20-minute phone call.**

So *"I don't want to do it"* cannot be the reason to skip the call — **the call is the
cheaper of the two things he has just agreed to do**, and it's the one that produces the
artifact he says he'd trust. The plan he's picked is *more* work for him **and** yields a
*less* trustworthy map. Ten minutes of listening before he opens the editor makes the
finalize step both easier and honest. `[inferred, strong]`

§2's trap stands unchanged: **only she can source it; anyone can scribe it.** The update
doesn't dissolve the anchoring horn — it **relocates** it. Phase 1 is now the *only*
omission-detector in the design, which makes it more valuable than it was an hour ago,
not less.

### 10.7 Revised smallest first step — this week

1. **Capture fix ships.** Unchanged. Precondition.
2. **Per-name provenance** — now an *input* to step 1, not hygiene. 5 min, Paul-only.
3. **10 minutes of Phase 1 BEFORE he opens the zone editor.** The one change to his plan.
4. **Then he finalizes** — against her nouns, tagging each zone `heard-from-her` /

5. **Then the confirm is reconciliation** — *"tell me what each of these is"*, not

6. **Ship photos on confirm cards.** Independent, verified defect.
