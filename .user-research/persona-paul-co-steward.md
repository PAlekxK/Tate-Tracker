# Paul — Co-steward and builder-user

The other half of the joint primary user pair. Paul is unusual in this persona set because he is simultaneously the user, the builder, and the person specifying the work — the artifact has to separate "Paul the user with a job to do on the property" from "Paul the strategist deciding what Tate Tracker should be."

## Situation

`[inferred]` — Atlanta-based; visits the property periodically rather than living on it. Doesn't see day-to-day changes the way Mom does, so his relationship to the property is partly continuity-of-memory (what was true last visit) and partly remote-stewardship (what's happening between visits). He brings the technical capacity to build the app and a real personal love of the place; those two motivations reinforce rather than conflict.

## Job-to-be-done

`[inferred]` — Steward the property *with* Mom as a real partner — not by managing the place from afar, but by being present in it through the app between visits, and by being a more capable hands-on contributor when on-site. Also: enjoy and deepen his own appreciation of the place (wildlife, plants, watershed, dark sky, Cherokee history) — the research-resource accumulation in the project is itself evidence of this job.

## Triggers

`[inferred]` — Visits to the property; a research thread he wants to follow (Cherokee land history, Etowah darters, native keystones); a build session where Tate Tracker is his current project focus; Mom mentioning something happening at the property. The build-the-app loop and the use-the-app loop reinforce each other — improving the app *is* a way of stewarding the property.

## Constraints

- `[validated]` — Project tone is load-bearing: field journal, not task manager. No urgency language. This is Paul's own design constraint and he has held it consistently. (Source: Tate Tracker CLAUDE.md; foundation notes.)
- `[inferred]` — Time is finite — Tate Tracker shares Paul's attention with consulting work, Bolo Boys, and his Claude Code meta-project. The app needs to pay off relative to the time he puts in.
- `[inferred]` — As a builder-user, he risks over-building for himself and under-building for Mom. He has explicitly named Mom as the make-or-break user, which is a guardrail against this — but the bias is structural and the artifacts should help check it.
- `[inferred]` — Mobile and desktop both. He'll use it on the property (mobile) and at his desk in Atlanta (desktop) while researching and building.

## Definition of success

`[inferred]` — Two things, both have to be true: (1) Mom uses it unprompted and it earns its place in her routine; (2) the app deepens his own relationship to the property — he learns about it, notices more on visits, and the research threads (history, ecology, watershed) feel integrated rather than parked. Failure mode: the app becomes a project he tinkers with but neither he nor Mom actually opens to *use*.

## Anti-persona

`[inferred]` — The hobbyist developer building for the pleasure of building, with no actual user adoption check. Paul has structurally guarded against this by naming Mom as make-or-break — the anti-persona is the version of himself that forgets that.

Also anti: the property-management professional who wants a maintenance system of record. Paul has access to that style of thinking from his consulting work and has deliberately *not* applied it here. Tate Tracker is field journal, not asset management.
