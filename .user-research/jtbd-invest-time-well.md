# When I'm steward of a place I love and time is finite, I want to know what's worth doing this month and what's worth noticing, so I can invest my time well — without the place becoming work.

This is the joint job for Paul and Mom. The dual nature of Tate Tracker — stewardship + appreciation — collapses into one underlying motive: invest finite time well on a place that matters, and let the investing of time itself be part of the enjoyment, not in tension with it.

## Forces

### Push (pain in current state)
- `[inferred]` — Without a shared reference, knowledge of the place is held in heads (Mom's especially) and in scattered notes. You can't easily ask "what should I be doing this month?" or "what's that bird?" and get a property-specific answer.
- `[inferred]` — Stewardship guesswork costs time and confidence both. Not knowing when to prune a hydrangea or which fertilizer the river birch wants means either skipping the action or doing it nervously. Both are unsatisfying.
- `[inferred]` — Appreciation is fragmentary — you notice a bird, then forget by next week. The signal of "what's actually happening on this land right now" has no place to land.

### Pull (attraction to new solution)
- `[inferred]` — A property-specific field journal that knows *this* place: this Bortle 3 sky, this Etowah watershed, these specific plants Mom and Paul have planted, this Cherokee-land history, these PWS readings.
- `[inferred]` — Confidence-building information delivered in a non-task-y way: "What's happening in May" rather than "17 actions due." Knowing without being managed.
- `[inferred]` — The pleasure of depth — the app makes the place richer to be in, because it surfaces context (the lake was built by Col. Sam Tate in 1929; the darters in our creek exist nowhere else) that turns time-on-the-property into something more than chores plus enjoyment-of-views.
- `[inferred]` — **"Something interesting to read" — and a soft preview of tomorrow.** In bed with coffee or winding down at night, the app competes with whatever else is on her phone in leisure mode. The pull here is leisure-reading pleasure *and* low-commitment previewing of what's coming on the property (what to do, what's blooming, what the weather will do). The bed/coffee context can serve **both** jobs lightly — appreciation as leisure-reading, and stewardship as forward-look — so the dual jobs may be intertwined even here, not cleanly split. (Source: Paul direct 2026-05-11.)

### Anxieties (worries holding them back)
- `[inferred]` — **The app itself becomes a chore.** Tate Tracker is supposed to feel like a field journal; if it starts feeling like another inbox or task list, the appreciation half of the job dies and Mom stops opening it. The pull toward stewardship can't override the push of "this is one more obligation."
- `[validated, inline]` — **Being too careful causes you to miss the right moments.** *"We also don't wanna be so gun shy about [pruning/fertilizing] that we don't do things we could be doing to make them more beautiful."* — Paul, 2026-05-11. The anxiety isn't only "did I do it wrong?" — it's also "did I hesitate past the window?" Confidence-building has to cut both ways: give permission to act, not only warnings about getting it wrong.

### Habits / inertia (what makes change hard)
- `[inferred]` — The existing system works. Mom holds knowledge of the property in her head, Paul holds research in scattered notes, both rely on memory of past seasons. It's imperfect but it functions — so the app has to offer enough to overcome "we've always managed without it."
- `[inferred]` — Other apps on Mom's phone have already taught her what "another app I'm supposed to use" feels like. Tate Tracker has to land categorically differently — not "but better."

## Performers

- [Mom](./persona-mom.md) — make-or-break user; the appreciation half of the job is especially load-bearing for her, and the stewardship half has to earn its place without slipping into task-management language.
- [Paul](./persona-paul-co-steward.md) — co-steward and builder; the depth/appreciation half (research, history, ecology) is the one he's actively feeding through the project's research library. His version of the job leans slightly more toward "make this place richer to know" than Mom's version.
