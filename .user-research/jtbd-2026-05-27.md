# Mom's jobs — first telemetry-grounded read

A grounded look at the jobs Mom is actually hiring Fernwood to do, based on six days of real usage data. Six jobs that the dashboard IS serving (1–5 validated by telemetry; 6 inferred). Then four candidate jobs that the dashboard ISN'T serving yet — input for the parallel ai-advisor session on "incorporating more info/research."

The framing is deliberately concrete and behavioral. Where Paul has stated a hypothesis but telemetry hasn't confirmed it, the job is tagged `inferred` and the gap is named. Where telemetry contradicts a prior hypothesis, the older framing is called out.

This artifact sits inside the parent JTBD (`jtbd-invest-time-well.md`) and alongside the Phase E inner job (`jtbd-talk-to-the-property.md`). It doesn't replace those — it refines them with first-real-usage signal.

## Jobs the dashboard IS serving (top 5 by telemetry signal)

### Job 1 — Scan the cards and see what the place is doing today

> **When** I open my phone in the morning or wind down at night, **I want to** look at the place at a glance — what plants are active, what the weather's like, what wildlife is around — **so I can** stay connected to what's happening at Fernwood without having to be on-site.

**Evidence:** `[validated]` — 341 events in 6 days on her device; `card_section_viewed` is 194 of those (57%). She is scanning, not deep-diving. Plants and Weather are tied at 60 views each on her device; Wildlife at 54; Celestial at 47; Property at 45. The dashboard's at-a-glance design is being used as designed. The card-popularity distribution is roughly uniform across plant/weather/wildlife/celestial/property — meaning ALL the main surfaces are getting attention, not just one. Source: `.audit/2026-05-26-telemetry-rollup.md` card-popularity + event-types tables.

**Forces:**
- **Push** `[validated]` — Without a single surface, the place lived in her head and her memory. The dashboard externalizes it. She's reaching for it ~4.5x/day.
- **Pull** `[validated]` — The card-based, scannable, no-obligation layout is doing its job. The lack of task-manager language matters here — she's "looking out at the land," not "checking her notifications."
- **Anxieties** `[inferred]` — That the next iteration adds a surface that introduces obligation. Telemetry doesn't show this yet because there's nothing obligation-shaped on the screen. It would show up as a drop in session frequency the first time it appeared.
- **Habits** `[validated]` — She has formed a daily pattern in 6 days. That's the strongest possible adoption signal.

### Job 2 — Come back to something I read before, without having to organize it

> **When** an entry, a plant, or a wildlife note caught my attention last time, **I want to** find it again easily, **so I can** keep the thread of the place in my head without building a system around it.

**Evidence:** `[validated]` — 55 `entry_revisited` events on her device (104 across all devices), 0 stars. She is revisiting at scale; she is not curating with the explicit star. The 17:1 revisit-to-save ratio confirms what the eval rubric framed as the curation hypothesis: saved entries ARE the personal library. The act of curation is *coming back*, not *marking*. Source: `.audit/2026-05-26-telemetry-rollup.md` curation section.

**Forces:**
- **Push** `[validated]` — Memory alone doesn't hold the thread across days; she does want to revisit.
- **Pull** `[validated]` — Field-notes card has the highest expand-rate of any card (18/43 = 42%). When she opens that surface, she opens it deeply.
- **Anxieties** `[inferred]` — That her "this matters" signal is being silently lost. From a system-design perspective the revisit *is* the signal — but she doesn't necessarily know that the dashboard knows she came back. Worth a T+30 conversation: "do you ever wish you could mark an entry as important?" If yes, the star failed at discoverability. If no, the star is conceptually wrong.
- **Habits** `[validated]` — Revisiting is now her habit. The mental model is "I'll just come back to it," not "I'll save it for later."

**Open contradiction worth naming:** The almanac save model PRD assumed a "this matters" star/pin affordance would surface valuable entries. Six days of zero stars on 104 revisits says: she doesn't think in star-marked-objects; she thinks in places-she-comes-back-to. Three explanations (invisible / wrong model / redundant) — see persona open questions.

### Job 3 — Ask the property to identify something I can't name on the spot

> **When** I see something at Fernwood I can't identify — a flower I don't recognize, a plant that looks unfamiliar — **I want to** point my phone at it and ask, **so I can** know what I'm looking at without having to be a naturalist.

**Evidence:** `[validated]` — 2 conversations on her device, both with image attachments, 1 `species_id_confirmed`, 1 `species_promoted` (Spiderwort, 5/22). She has actually used the photo + ID + promote loop end-to-end. Phase F Option C is doing its job. The 2026-05-16 Butterfly Weed loop (real Claude conversation that became canon) and the 2026-05-22 Spiderwort promotion are the canonical examples of this job in action. Source: `.audit/2026-05-26-telemetry-rollup.md` Garden Guru engagement + Phase F Option C confirmation.

**Forces:**
- **Push** `[validated, inline]` — From eval-garden-guru Q4: Mom is already a Claude-power-user with the photos-for-ID workflow. The job exists; the question was only whether Fernwood would serve it better than her existing Claude flow.
- **Pull** `[validated]` — Pre-loaded property context (Q2 in the rubric: "*you can pull this up without providing all the context… and just pick up the phone and ask what is this*"). For Spiderwort 5/22, the workflow worked: photo → ID → confirm → promote to canon. That's the killer use case landing.
- **Anxieties** `[validated, inline]` — Voice-but-cold (Q6) and invented-plants-on-property (Q8b hard fail). The eval rubric tracks these explicitly.
- **Habits** `[inferred]` — 2 conversations in 6 days is "tried and worked," not "habit." Whether this becomes pattern depends on how often she encounters something she can't name. May be seasonal.

### Job 4 — Read at a size and contrast my eyes can handle without glasses

> **When** I want to look at the dashboard but I don't have my reading glasses (in bed, on the porch, in the morning), **I want to** be able to read it anyway, **so I can** participate in the app without having to find my glasses first.

**Evidence:** `[validated]` — 12 `text_size_changed` events on her device, the ONLY device that touched the A/A+ toggle Paul shipped 5/22 for this exact constraint. She is reaching for the affordance, repeatedly. Source: `.audit/2026-05-26-telemetry-rollup.md` event-types table.

**Forces:**
- **Push** `[validated]` — Stated directly by Paul on 2026-05-22 and now behaviorally confirmed.
- **Pull** `[validated]` — The A/A+ toggle is doing its job; she found it and uses it.
- **Anxieties** `[inferred]` — That a future iteration breaks the readability she has now. Any small-body-copy, faint-helper-text, or label-only-routing pattern triggers this — even if the toggle is still present. Per `project_fernwood_mom_reading_accessibility.md`: meaning has to be carried by icon + size + color + position, not by label text alone.
- **Habits** `[validated]` — 12 events in 6 days. It's part of how she uses the app — likely toggled-up at the start of a session in bed and toggled-down on the porch in better light. Worth checking the event-timing distribution in a future rollup if Paul wants to understand the trigger.

### Job 5 — Steward what's on the property by promoting what I find (low-key co-authorship)

> **When** I've confirmed what something is — a Spiderwort I photographed, the autumn bentgrass I want to consider planting — **I want to** see it become part of the actual record, **so I can** feel like my noticing is contributing to what Fernwood knows about itself.

**Evidence:** `[validated]` — 1 `species_promoted` on her device (Spiderwort, 5/22), 1 on the other unmapped iPhone. Plus: the autumn bentgrass entry in the new "Worth considering" card (shipped 5/26) is explicitly labeled "Mom's pick." She has contributed a candidate to the canon. The act of promotion is now a real interaction shape, not a hypothetical. Source: `.audit/2026-05-26-telemetry-rollup.md` Phase F Option C section + CLAUDE.md "Worth Considering" candidates card section.

**Forces:**
- **Push** `[inferred]` — She lives the property day-to-day in a way Paul doesn't (per persona); without a way to promote what she finds, that knowledge stays in her head. The promote loop is the first real way she can write to the canon.
- **Pull** `[inferred — strengthened by Mom's-pick badge]` — Co-authorship that's visible. The "Mom's pick" badge on autumn bentgrass is a small but real signal that her contribution shows up in the dashboard's voice, not as anonymous data. This is the kind of warmth the field-journal voice was set up for.
- **Anxieties** `[inferred]` — That a wrong-ID promote becomes part of the canon and looks like she got it wrong. The two-step confirm flow (Step A "does that look right?" → Step B "worth adding?") addresses this. Telemetry suggests she's clearing both steps cleanly.
- **Habits** `[inferred]` — Too early. 1 promotion in 6 days. The shape of the habit will depend on how often she encounters new things.

### Job 6 — Take a moment that's just for the place, not for any task

> **When** I want to enjoy Fernwood without it being about doing or remembering anything, **I want to** scroll through what's blooming, what's outside, what the sky looks like tonight, **so I can** feel connected to the place even when I'm not there.

**Evidence:** `[inferred — supported but not isolated by telemetry]` — Celestial card gets 47 views on her device (more than Property card at 45). Celestial is purely contemplative — nothing to do with stewardship at all. She's reaching for it. Cannot be cleanly isolated from Job 1 (scan the cards) without a per-card session-shape analysis (do Celestial sessions look different from Plants sessions?), but the appreciation half of the joint job is alive. The "leisure-mode reading session" framing from Paul direct 2026-05-11 is consistent with what telemetry shows. Source: `.audit/2026-05-26-telemetry-rollup.md` card-popularity table.

**Forces:**
- **Push** `[inferred]` — The other phone apps in her morning-coffee rotation don't carry the place with them. Fernwood does.
- **Pull** `[inferred]` — Field-journal voice, Bortle 3 dark sky context, the Crimson serif on plant names, the warmth of "Notes on the estate" — the things that aren't task-manager.
- **Anxieties** `[inferred]` — That a feature added in a future iteration shifts the app toward managing and away from looking. Same as Job 1.
- **Habits** `[inferred — strengthening]` — 6 days of daily use isn't enough to call it habit-as-leisure-reading. But it's enough to say the appreciation half of the joint job is real, not theoretical.

**Why this is `inferred` and not `validated`:** telemetry shows what gets viewed, not why. The contemplative interpretation is plausible but not isolated. A T+30 question — *"think about the last time you opened Fernwood when you weren't looking for anything specific — what did you do?"* — would do the work.

## Jobs the dashboard ISN'T serving yet (input for ai-advisor session)

These are candidate jobs surfaced by gaps in the telemetry, not by direct Mom-statement. They're tagged `inferred` because none come from Mom's words — they come from the user-researcher's read of what a property field-journal could do that this one currently doesn't. They are sparks for the ai-advisor "incorporate more info/research" question, not commitments.

### Job 7 (unserved) — Know what to look for this week

> **When** the seasons shift and the property is doing different things, **I want to** know what to expect to see in the next week or two, **so I can** notice the right things before they pass and feel oriented to the calendar of the place.

**Why this is a gap:** `[inferred]` — The dashboard has a "this month" view in Plants (60 views from her — heavy use). But there's no forward-looking, week-resolution surface that says "*here's what's coming up*" across plants + wildlife + celestial + weather. Today's surface is mostly present-tense ("what's blooming") or month-tense ("May care"). The week-tense gap is real.

**Why it matters:** `[inferred]` — Maps to the eval-rubric Q1 win pattern of "one well-placed question per week" — but flipped. Instead of Mom asking, the dashboard could surface. Risk: this verges on task-manager-by-stealth ("here's what you should be looking for"). The field-journal version is closer to "this is when last year's hummingbirds arrived; watch the feeder this week." The voice rule from `project_tate_tracker_observations_feedback_loop.md` applies — "you noted X last year" cadence, memory not database.

**What real research would look like:** ask Mom in the T+30 interview: *"think about something you almost missed at Fernwood — a bloom that went past, a bird that came and went. What would have helped you not miss it?"*

### Job 8 (unserved) — Compare this year to last year on the same calendar

> **When** something happens at the property — first hummingbird at the feeder, hydrangea blooming, frost — **I want to** know how this year compares to last year and the year before, **so I can** feel like the property has memory and I'm not starting from scratch every season.

**Why this is a gap:** `[inferred]` — The structured journal accumulates dated entries (6 `field_note_saved` events in the window, 4 on her device). After a year of use, this is the substrate for year-over-year comparison. The dashboard doesn't yet surface it that way. Today, an entry is a point-in-time note; tomorrow, the same date one year later could trigger "you noted the laurel opening April 25 last year — watch for it now." Phase G observations-as-knowledge-layer is exactly this thread (see CLAUDE.md "Forward direction" + memory `project_tate_tracker_observations_feedback_loop.md`). It's named, deferred, and waiting.

**Why it matters:** `[inferred]` — This is what turns the journal from a log into the *almanac* the card title now claims to be. It's also where Fernwood's wedge over Mom's existing Claude+photos workflow widens dramatically: Claude doesn't know what she observed last spring. Fernwood will.

**The constraint to honor:** voice rule — when a callout cites a past observation, it should sound like memory ("you noted X last year"), not like a database row ("OBSERVATION_2025-04-25 SPECIES=Mountain Laurel STATUS=blooming"). The risk is that the engineering naturally produces the second voice and someone has to translate.

**Timing note:** Phase G is gated on "observation set is rich enough (~50+ entries)." At 6 saved entries today, we're far from that — but the count understates the substrate, because every Garden Guru conversation, every species promotion, and every revisit is also signal Phase G could draw on.

### Job 9 (unserved) — Look something up that I had a question about last month

> **When** I had a question about Fernwood weeks ago and the answer came in a Garden Guru conversation, **I want to** find that answer again without remembering what day it was, **so I can** treat my own past questions as a personal library.

**Why this is a gap:** `[validated, by absence]` — From CLAUDE.md Phase E open iterations: *"Conversation browse UI. KV has all conversations; no UI to browse them yet. v2."* The conversations are persisted server-side; there's no surface to read them. If she had a question 3 weeks ago and the answer was good, she can't go back to it. Source: CLAUDE.md → "Open Phase E iterations."

**Why it matters:** `[inferred]` — Per eval-rubric Q5: the "personal library accumulates" win at 3 months depends on this. Conversations are currently write-only from her side; the value compounds only if she can read them back. This is a structural gap, not a stylistic one — it's holding back the project's long-arc value.

**Open design question:** what's the right surface? A "past conversations" list is conventional but conventional-chatbot — and that's exactly what eval-rubric Q-anxiety #1 wants to avoid (Garden Guru should not feel like ChatGPT). Possible alternatives: conversations get auto-tagged to a card (a Spiderwort conversation lives on the Spiderwort entry), or get surfaced contextually ("you asked about this once — here's what Garden Guru said"). The voice rule again: memory-shaped, not database-shaped.

### Job 10 (unserved) — Show this to a visiting friend or family member without their having access

> **When** someone visits Fernwood and asks about the place, **I want to** show them what we know about it — the plants, the wildlife, the history — **so I can** share the place with them without explaining everything from scratch.

**Why this is a gap:** `[inferred]` — Today the app is family-only (Paul + Mom + brother). There's no read-only / guest / show-this surface. The use case is real: a friend visiting, a neighbor curious, a family member visiting for the weekend. The app is rich enough that showing it would be a good way to introduce someone to the place — but the surface for "share this view briefly" doesn't exist.

**Why it might NOT matter (counter-argument):** `[inferred]` — Paul has been deliberate about scoping Fernwood as personal, hyper-personal, not for the public. A "share with friend" feature could pull the project toward a generic-property-app shape Paul has actively avoided. The right test isn't "would Mom use this" but "would Paul be comfortable with this surface existing?" That's a strategy question, not a research question.

**Worth raising with Paul** before treating it as a real candidate. It's the easiest of these four to over-build.

## Paul-as-secondary-user — the verdict

`[validated]` — **Paul has effectively stopped being a regular Fernwood user on his mapped iPhone.** Per telemetry: `d-fxeb35uh-...` (Paul's mapped iPhone) shows 7 sessions / 53 events with last activity 2026-05-21. That's six days of silence on the device Paul uses to use Fernwood as a user (not as a builder). His laptop device (`d-avslqpyd-...`) is active (32 sessions / 230 events, last seen 5/26) but that's the *builder* — that's how he tests, deploys, edits. Source: `.audit/2026-05-26-telemetry-rollup.md` engagement-by-device table.

`[inferred]` — There are two readings of this and they have very different design implications:

1. **Reading A: Paul's iPhone shifted, the user is still using.** The second unmapped iPhone (`d-szqlt0h7-...`, 16 sessions / 135 events) could be Paul's iPhone after a Safari clear or a different profile. If so, Paul-as-user is alive — just on a different device ID. This is the testable reading: Paul checks his iPhone Safari profile / clears history pattern. Worth resolving before designing for it. (Telemetry rollup names this as the highest-value missing data: `tools/people.json` mapping.)

2. **Reading B: Paul-as-user has effectively become Paul-as-builder, and Mom is the user.** If `d-szqlt0h7-...` is someone else (brother? Mom's iPad? a second device of Mom's?) then Paul has stopped consuming his own product. This is consistent with the builder-user anti-persona named in `persona-paul-co-steward.md` ("the hobbyist developer building for the pleasure of building, with no actual user adoption check"). The guardrail Paul put in (Mom as make-or-break) is still working — Mom is adopting — but Paul-the-user vanished.

**The design implication depends on which reading is correct:**

- If A: continue designing for the three-performer model (Paul-mobile, Paul-desktop, Mom). The eval rubric stands.
- If B: collapse Paul-mobile from "real performer" to "occasional performer / builder validation only." The eval rubric for Paul-mobile becomes a check Paul does deliberately, not a use mode that emerges from his daily behavior. The Phase F killer-use-case framing (photo + ID + property-anchored answer, on-property) loses its primary performer; it's serving Mom alone now. That's not bad — it just changes the design weight.

**Either reading is fine.** What's not fine is leaving the question unresolved while continuing to design for Reading A by default. The action: Paul resolves `d-szqlt0h7-...` ownership at next opportunity. Either updates `tools/people.json` to map it (Reading A) or accepts that he's primarily a builder now (Reading B).

## T+30 Mom-interview prep — additions surfaced by telemetry

The meta-feedback validation question from PHASE_E_MVP.md is locked: *"Have you ever typed something into Fernwood that was about the app itself, not the property? What did you do with it?"* That stands.

Telemetry surfaces seven additional questions worth carrying. They are Mom-Test-safe: past-behavior, no leading framing, no hypothetical-future asks. Pick the ones that fit the conversation; don't read all seven in sequence.

1. **On the star (zero use in 55 revisits):**
   > *"Think about the last entry — a plant note, a bird note, whatever — that mattered to you. What did you do with it on your phone?"*
   
   Distinguishes invisible-star (she didn't see the affordance) from wrong-model-star (entries aren't curatable objects to her) from redundant-star (revisiting IS her marking). The answer should drive the kill/move/replace decision on the star.

2. **On the 2-turn ceiling on Garden Guru:**
   > *"Tell me about the last question you asked Garden Guru. What did you do with the answer?"*
   
   If she says "I read it and put my phone down" → 2-turn is enough. If she says "I wanted to ask more but didn't know how" → discoverability issue. If she says "I went and looked at the plant" → great, but Guru is now competing with the dashboard cards rather than chaining to a follow-up.

3. **On the readability work:**
   > *"You changed the text size a few times this week — do you remember doing that? What made you want to?"*
   
   Validates the trigger (in bed, on porch, etc.) — and may surface other accessibility tweaks she'd want.

4. **On the appreciation job:**
   > *"Think about the last time you opened Fernwood when you weren't looking for anything specific. What did you do?"*
   
   Validates Job 6 (Take a moment that's just for the place). Distinguishes leisure-reading from forward-looking from genuinely-aimless scrolling.

5. **On the unserved Job 7 (know what to look for):**
   > *"Think about something at Fernwood you almost missed this spring — a bloom that went past, a bird that came and went. What would have helped you not miss it?"*
   
   Tests whether the week-resolution forward-look gap is real to her.

6. **On the unserved Job 8 (year-over-year memory):**
   > *"Have you ever wondered when something happened at the property last year — when the first hummingbird showed up, when something started blooming?"*
   
   Tests whether the Phase G observation-as-memory pull is real. If yes, Phase G priority goes up. If no, it may be more a Paul-want than a Mom-want.

7. **On the unserved Job 9 (look up a past question):**
   > *"Has there ever been a question you asked Garden Guru, then forgot the answer, and wished you could find it again?"*
   
   Tests whether the conversation-browse gap is real-felt. If she says "no, I just ask it again," then the browse gap is a Paul-want, not a Mom-want. That meaningfully de-prioritizes the v2 Phase E iteration.

**One soft suggestion for the interview overall:** the previous T+30 design assumed one conversation, with the meta-feedback question central. Telemetry surfaces enough genuine gaps that the conversation can do more — but only if Paul can manage the time without it becoming an interrogation. Three of these seven (the star, the 2-turn, the appreciation job) are high-priority and Mom-Test-clean. The others can wait if the conversation runs long.
