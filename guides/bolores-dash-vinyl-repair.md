# Bolores — Dashboard Vinyl Tear Repair

*1989 Ford Bronco Eddie Bauer · small tear in the padded dash skin · chestnut interior*

The '89 dash is a **foam-backed vinyl pad**. A small tear in the vinyl skin is a repair-in-place job, not a replacement. The whole point of doing it now is to converge with the interior refinish: **repair the substrate, and let the chestnut paint/dye pass handle the color** along with the rest of the interior (so it disappears instead of being a patch you can spot).

> **First, decide which job this is:**
> - **Small tear / split / gouge in the skin** → repair below. This is the expected case.
> - **The pad is cracked or sun-split across a large area** (common on 35-year-old dashes) → the durable fix is a molded **dash cap** (Coverlay / LMC — an ABS shell that glues over the whole pad), not a hundred little repairs. Note it and decide separately. For *one small tear*, the cap is overkill.

---

## What you'll need
- **Vinyl/leather repair kit** with **heat-cure flexible compound + grain papers** (Coconix, Permatex Vinyl & Leather, or a 3M kit). The grain papers are the key — they're what makes the repair vanish.
- **Sub-patch backing fabric** (canvas/denim scrap, or the kit's piece) for any tear with an opening.
- **Isopropyl alcohol** (90%+) / vinyl prep cleaner, clean rags.
- **Fresh razor blade**, small spatula/spreader.
- **Low-heat tool** — the kit's curing iron, or a household iron on low through the grain paper.
- **For color *only if the dash isn't going through the interior refinish*:** SEM Color Coat (vinyl & plastic adhesion paint) matched to chestnut, plus a matte/satin clear to match the dash's low sheen.

---

## Steps

1. **Clean it.** Wipe the tear and the area around it with IPA until the rag comes away clean — years of skin oil + dash dressing (Armor-All) will stop anything from bonding. Let it flash off.
2. **Trim the loose edges.** With a fresh blade, shave off any frayed/curled vinyl so the torn edges lie flat. You want clean edges, not fuzz.
3. **Back it with a sub-patch.** If the tear is open (you can see foam through it), slip a thin backing fabric *under* the vinyl through the opening, dab adhesive, and press the vinyl edges down onto it. This gives the compound something solid to bridge — never fill over bare foam or open air.
4. **Fill in thin layers.** Spread the flexible compound into the tear in **thin coats**, letting each set before the next, building up **just to flush** with the surrounding surface. Resist overfilling — a proud repair is obvious and sands poorly.
5. **Set the grain.** While the top coat is still workable, lay a **grain paper that matches the dash's pebble texture** over it and heat-set per the kit (low heat). This embosses the same grain into your repair so it blends into the surrounding texture. **This step is the make-or-break for invisibility** — take time matching the grain pattern.
6. **Cure fully** before touching or coloring (follow the kit's time).
7. **Color — two paths:**
   - **Dash is part of the interior refinish (preferred):** *stop here.* Hand it off cured, grained, and flush; it gets sprayed/dyed chestnut with the rest of the interior, so the color matches perfectly and there's nothing to blend.
   - **Dash is staying as-is:** color-match with **SEM Color Coat** (or matching vinyl dye) to chestnut — light coats, build slowly. Finish with a **matte/satin clear** if needed; dashes are deliberately low-gloss so they don't glare in the windshield.

---

## Cautions
- **Use FLEXIBLE compound and adhesion-promoting color — never rigid filler or regular paint.** The dash flexes and bakes in the sun; anything rigid will crack and peel.
- **Test color on a hidden spot** (under the lip) before committing — chestnut is easy to miss warm-vs-cool.
- **Keep the sheen low** — a glossy patch on a matte dash stands out as much as a color mismatch, and gloss glares in the glass.
