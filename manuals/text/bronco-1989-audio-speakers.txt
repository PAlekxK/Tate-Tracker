===============================================================================
WHAT IS ACTUALLY INSTALLED
===============================================================================
Four speakers, two different sizes. Installed ~Feb 2026 in one session together
with the head unit. All four run OFF THE HEAD UNIT — the truck's pre-existing
external amplifier did not work and was pulled.

  FRONT (both doors) ....... Infinity KAPPA 63XF   — 6.5 in (165 mm) coaxial
  REAR ..................... Infinity KAPPA 86CFX  — 6 x 8 in coaxial

The front door openings are 6.5 in ROUND. This was established by photo
diagnosis during the install after a 6x8 was initially assumed; the mixed
front-6.5 / rear-6x8 arrangement is a consequence of that finding, not a
preference. The speakers they replaced were Pioneer TS-A1674R in front.

Front speakers are mounted on NVX XBAF65 6.5 in silicone speaker baffles.

Power handling ............... 85 W RMS / 255 W peak
NOMINAL IMPEDANCE ............ 3 ohms          <-- see IMPEDANCE WARNING below
Sensitivity .................. 93 dB @ 2.83 V
Frequency response ........... 45 Hz – 40 kHz
Woofer ....................... Plus One+ glass-fiber cone
Tweeter ...................... edge-driven silk dome, 40 kHz high-resolution
Tweeter level control ........ push-button on the angled tweeter,
                               0 dB or -3 dB output level

Sources: sonicelectronix.com item 183273; shop.singhelectronics.ca;
creativeaudio.net/kappa63xf/. These three agree on power, impedance and
sensitivity.

⭐ THE TWEETER LEVEL SWITCH IS A TEARDOWN-WINDOW ITEM. It is a physical button
on the speaker, so reaching it means the door card comes off. The door cards
are already coming off for the panel repair + sound deadening (restoration
Phase 2), and that is the cheap moment to audition 0 dB vs -3 dB. After the
doors go back together it is a whole disassembly to change a setting.

Source: th.infinityspeakers.com/KAPPA+86CFX_.html (Infinity regional site —
the only Infinity-owned page that served content).

NOTE ON THE TWEETER LEVEL CONVENTION: the 63XF is published as 0 / -3 dB
(cut) and the 86CFX as +0 / +3 dB (boost). Both are reproduced here as the
vendor states them rather than normalised, because which direction the switch
moves changes what you would do with it. Verify against the speaker itself
before relying on the sign.


===============================================================================
⚠️⚠️  IMPEDANCE WARNING — THE ONE THING TO KNOW
===============================================================================
BOTH SPEAKER PAIRS SIT BELOW THE HEAD UNIT'S RATED OUTPUT IMPEDANCE.

  RetroSound Motor 2B rated output impedance ............ 4 ohms
  Kappa 63XF (front) ................................... 3 ohms
  Kappa 86CFX (rear) ................................... 2.5 ohms

RetroSound's own Motor 2B user manual names this exact condition as a fault
cause. From the Frequently Asked Questions, p.19, under "I have all 4 speakers
connected to your radio and the display is lit up like it is working, but
there's no sound coming out":

    "Speaker impedance below 4 ohms. Older speakers are usually not
     compatible with modern electronics."

And the Troubleshooting table, p.18, lists "Internal amp in protection mode"
as a cause of "No sound heard from unit but unit lights up."

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT MEAN:
  • It is NOT a prediction of failure. The system is installed and playing and
    Paul's own assessment is that it "sounds good as-is" (recorded Feb 2026).
    A 3-ohm load on a 4-ohm-rated amp is a mild overdraw; 2.5 ohms is not mild.
  • It DOES mean the head unit's internal amplifier is being asked for more
    current than it is specified to deliver, continuously, on all four
    channels — and the rear pair is the worse offender.
  • It IS an independent argument for the external amp, separate from any
    argument about loudness. An outboard amplifier rated for 2-ohm loads would
    be operating INSIDE its specification driving these speakers, which the
    Motor 2B is not. The amp stops being a want and becomes the thing that
    puts the load where it belongs.
  • ⛔ BEFORE BUYING ANY AMPLIFIER: confirm 2-ohm stability. A 4-ohm-only amp
    reproduces this same problem with a bigger power supply behind it.

If the radio ever cuts out when warm or at volume, this is the first thing to
suspect — not the wiring, not the head unit.
