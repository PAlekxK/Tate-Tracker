## 1 · ⭐ THE HELD ITEM — "we work out your weather and what grows there"

**Current** (`onboarding/index.html:533`, the flagship s2 disclosure `wide-eyed` called *"the bar"*):

> We work out your weather and what grows there from this address. Paul — who built this — is the only
> other person who sees it. You can fix it before saving.

**Measured:** weather half delivered and verified. What-grows half delivered to **nobody** — `frost` 0 ·
`hardiness` 0 · `growing season` 0 · `zone` 0 across 711 strings, 7 runs.

### 1a · ⛔ The recommendation Paul will not expect: do not ship the journey line

**"We'll keep exploring it together" should not go on this screen**, and my reasons are four:

1. **Could-be-anyone.** A stranger at any wellness app writes that sentence. It fails the first test in
   the library, on the one screen four seats named as the best writing in the product.
2. **It answers a question nobody is holding.** s2's disclosure has exactly one job — *why are you asking
   for my address*. A journey claim is a claim about the relationship, thirty seconds in, from a reader
   deciding whether to type where she lives.
3. **It conscripts the reader.** *Together* makes her a participant in work she has not agreed to do.
4. ⭐ **It is unfalsifiable, which is Paul's own trap, stated exactly.** A promise with no object can
   never be discharged and therefore never retired. That is not a wording risk; it is the mechanism by
   which the sentence becomes permanent.

**The journey is real. The sentence saying so is the problem.** The fix is not to soften the promise
into vagueness — it is to make the promise **smaller, dated, and separately checkable**: say what the
address delivers *now*, name what it does not deliver *yet*, and move the forward-looking half onto a
surface that has a clock on its face (the ribbon, §5).

Fernwood already owns the device for this and it is human-gated: the ranked-interests row ships
`(an idea — not built yet)` (`estate/index.html:428`). Reuse that honesty, don't invent a register.

### 1b · DRAFT LINES — s2 disclosure

**A — RECOMMENDED (31 words; current is 33, so this is *shorter* and says more):**

> **This is how I work out your weather. Frost dates and what grows there come later. Paul — who built
> this — is the only other person who sees it. You can put it right on the next screen.**

- Two claims where there was one, both separately true and separately checkable.
- *use* · *who sees it* · *reversibility* all survive whole (`[[feedback_every_ask_says_use_and_reversibility]]`).
- "come later" is the release condition's copy face — see §1c.

**B — one sentence, closest to the current shape:**

> I work out your weather from this address — frost dates and what grows there are still to come. Paul —
> who built this — is the only other person who sees it. You can put it right on the next screen.

**C — Paul's journey framing, executed as well as it can be. ⛔ I recommend against it:**

> This is the first thing I ask for and the most useful — your weather comes straight from it. What
> grows there follows once I've built it. Paul — who built this — is the only other person who sees it.

C drops the reversibility clause to stay under length. That alone disqualifies it.

### 1c · ⭐ THE RELEASE CONDITION THE WORDING COMMITS US TO

Paul asked what "later" implicitly promises. Precisely three things:

1. **A named deliverable.** The sentence names *frost dates* and *what grows there*. The debt is
   discharged when a household **that is not Fernwood** sees a frost date, zone or growing season on its
   own screen, derived from its own coordinates. Not when a garden card exists. Not when a plan says so.
2. **A falsifier that already exists and needs no new tooling.** `wide-eyed`'s walk already greps
   `frost` · `hardiness` · `growing season` · `zone`. Today: 0/0/0/0 across 711 strings. **The line
   becomes true the first time that grep is non-zero on a non-Fernwood household.** That is the release
   condition, in an instrument that is already running.
3. **A decay rule, and it is the half that is normally forgotten.** The moment the first frost date
   renders, *"come later"* must be re-cut to a plain delivery claim — *"This is how I work out your
   weather and what grows there."* A "later" that survives its own delivery is the stale-ribbon defect
   wearing setup-screen clothes.

⭐ **And the condition must be attached to the sentence, not to this plan.** Every load-bearing line in
these two files already carries its own reasoning as a comment beside it. This one should carry its own
**expiry**: a comment at `onboarding/index.html:533` naming the seat and the grep, so the next reader of
that line learns it is a dated debt rather than a settled sentence. That is what stops "later" becoming
permission never to deliver — not the wording, the citation next to the wording.

### 1d · What does NOT change

`estate/index.html:370` — *"A box number is where your post goes, not where your place is — so I can't
work out your weather or what grows there."* Still true, still the best line in the flow, still the one
place the product talks about *where your place is* rather than about land. Leave it.

## 3 · ⭐ "Stays on this phone for now — nobody else sees it."

**Current, three strings, one system:**

| where | string |
|---|---|
| `viewer.html:19609` composer sub-line | *Stays on this phone for now — nobody else sees it.* |
| `viewer.html:20723` pill | *On this phone* |
| `viewer.html:20472` banner | *Notes don't travel between devices yet.* |

**Verified true:** household instances run sync mode `household-local` and render **no** sync button
(`:20744`, *"no button to a door a household cannot open"*). One browser, one copy, no export.

### 3a · WHY IT ISN'T LEGIBLE — three named causes

1. **It answers two questions in one clause and the reader cannot tell which.** *"Stays on this phone"* is
   a **location** fact; *"nobody else sees it"* is a **privacy** fact. A reader takes the first half as a
   privacy reassurance and the second half confirms that reading — so the **consequence** (there is
   exactly one copy of this and nothing backs it up) never lands. **It is a disclosure that reads as a
   comfort.** That is precisely Paul's *"or what exactly does that mean?"*
2. **The four-clause rule is half-applied, and the wrong half.**
   `[[feedback_every_ask_says_use_and_reversibility]]` wants *use · not-use · who sees it · reversibility*
   under an input. This line carries **who sees it** and nothing else. The missing clause is not *used
   for* — it is the one this particular input raises: **what happens to it.** The fix is not more words;
   it is the right clause.
3. **"for now" carries the entire limitation and carries it invisibly** — two unstressed syllables mid-
   sentence. Its sibling, `Notes don't travel between devices yet`, was read by three seats as *"the app
   doesn't know I signed in"*. Same failure, one surface over.

### 3b · HOW THE TENSION RESOLVES

Say the consequence as a **property of the phone**, not as a **risk to the writing**. Reassurance first
and true (capture does not lie); location second, concrete enough that a reader can reach *"so if I lose
this phone…"* in one step by herself; limitation third, with *yet* carrying the roadmap.
**Describe, don't grade** applies directly: state what is, let the reader draw the consequence — but she
must be *able* to draw it, and today she cannot.

### 3c · DRAFT LINES — composer sub-line

**A — RECOMMENDED (15 words):**

> **"Saved here on this phone, and nowhere else yet. Nobody else can see it."**

- *Saved* leads — the true reassurance, first.
- *and nowhere else yet* is the consequence stated as a fact about **copies**, not a warning about loss.
  *yet* is the roadmap, with no date.
- *can* rather than *does* — she is asking about the guarantee, not the current headcount.

**B — shorter, sharper, riskier (13 words):**

> "Saved on this phone — the only copy, and only you can see it."

*The only copy* is the most legible form of the consequence in the product. ⚠️ It is also the closest
this gets to a warning, and on Mom it is the one likeliest to suppress writing. One string serves both
readers, so I recommend A.

**C — progressive disclosure (what the four-clause rule literally asks for). ⛔ Do not ship yet:**

> line: "Saved here on this phone. Only you can see it."
> tap: **"Where notes live ›"** → *"Your notes are kept in this browser, on this phone. They don't travel
> to your other devices yet, and nothing is sent anywhere. When they can follow you, it'll say so here."*

⚠️ Measured: Mom's depth-2 and depth-3 opens are **both zero** across the window — content behind a
disclosure is content she has not seen. C is right for a new household reader and wrong for her.

### 3d · The two siblings move in the same pass, or the surface says three things

- pill `On this phone` → **keep.** Shortest true label, and it now shares its first three words with the
  composer line.
- banner `Notes don't travel between devices yet.` → **"Notes stay on the phone you write them on. They
  can't travel between devices yet."** Where it is, then why. Today the banner opens by negating an
  expectation the reader may never have had.

### 3e · ⛔ WHAT COPY CANNOT FIX → engineering

There is one copy of a household's notes and no export. Copy can make that **legible**; it cannot make it
**safe**. If Paul wants this line to stop being uncomfortable, the durable answer is a door out — a member
sync path, or an export — not a better sentence. I am flagging it rather than writing a warmer line over it.

## 5 · ⭐ THE ACKNOWLEDGMENT RIBBON

Paul: reuse Mom's ribbon + feedback-box pattern to say we're working out weather and what-grows from the
address he gave, with a light *"OK sounds good"* acknowledgement — satisfying *use · not-use · who sees it
· reversibility*, without becoming a wall.

### 5a · ⭐ WHY THIS IS THE RIGHT HOME FOR §1's FORWARD HALF — and it answers his trap structurally

**A ribbon has a date on its face and is replaced by the next one. A setup-screen sentence is standing
text with no clock.** Move *"and what grows there is coming"* onto the ribbon and the promise acquires
three properties it cannot have on s2:

- it is **dated** — `arrivedAt` renders in the title;
- it is **replaced**, never accumulated (`paul-ruled 2026-07-22`);
- ⭐ and if it is **still saying *working on it* three ribbons later, that is visible on the card itself**,
  to Paul and to the reader, without anybody auditing anything.

That is the release condition made structural rather than editorial — the same shape as his own fixes
(the Recent-updates bridge; the `stakes` schema field).

### 5b · The ribbon's four rules bind this reuse, and one of them needs extending

Rules 1–4 live in `viewer.html:11845–11897`. Rule 1 names three heading shapes — her input **caused**
something · **settled** something · **nothing moved**. **This ribbon is a fourth shape: in progress.**
Proposed as a principle in §7 rather than smuggled in as a branch — three consecutive laps added a branch
to that derivation and `titlePhrase` exists precisely so the line is authored instead.

### 5c · ⭐ HOW THE FOUR CLAUSES SURVIVE WITHOUT A WALL

Paul's rule says all four, every time. Measured constraint: depth 2 and depth 3 are both **zero**, so
nothing can go behind a tap, and four sentences is a wall. My proposal is a **bounded exception with a
stated mechanism**, for him to rule on:

| clause | where it lands here | why |
|---|---|---|
| **USE** | the ribbon's **body is** the use statement | it says what the address is for by reporting what is being made from it. A separate sentence would be the card narrating itself. |
| **NOT-USE** | three words riding on the use clause — *"and nothing else"* | a not-use clause belongs under an **input**; a ribbon is not an input. Declined as a sentence, kept as a modifier. |
| **WHO SEES IT** | one clause, ~8 words, in the closing | said 30 seconds earlier at s2 — but **not** redundant for a reader on a later visit who skipped it. Carried once, short. |
| **REVERSIBILITY** | **the control**, not a sentence | standing rule: *"A standing 'you can change this' footer"* is forbidden; a control does that job. The closing points at `Change that ›`. |
