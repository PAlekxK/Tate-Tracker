## 1 · WHAT THE CONVERSATION IS — and it needs no new name

Tested against `feedback_reuse_vocabulary_before_adding_state` before proposing any term. **Two
existing words cover it exactly and a third would be drift:**

- **The gate.** The readiness proposal already calls `ready:` *"the gate. Nothing an agent writes."*
  The conversation is what happens **at** that gate. It is not a new ceremony; it is the gate, described.
- **The gate sweep.** `CYCLE-SPINE.md` § GATE-SWEEP AMENDMENT already defines the act of disposing
  fired gates with **four dispositions — act · fold · snooze · kill** — and already defines ⚡ as
  *"a gate has FIRED and nothing has disposed of it,"* explicitly **at two altitudes: loop level and
  item level.** An open question on a stamped-or-stampable plan **is an item-level fired gate.** It
  needs no fifth state and no new glyph.

> ### **THE STAMP GATE — a gate sweep over questions.**
> **Trigger** — not "a plan was drafted." **A step is next and a question blocks it.** (C4's stamp
> fired this way and its own text says so: *"Steps 1a–1d are unaffected by all four."*)
> **Input** — only the questions whose `blocks:` names a step that is next or already in flight.
> **Output** — a ruling block written **above** the questions in the plan's `## Open before stamping`
> section, **in Paul's own words, verbatim**, naming which questions it rules and which it does not.
> **Over when** — every question that blocked a now-next step carries a disposition. **NOT when every
> question is answered.** A plan with eleven questions parked at named later steps is a healthy plan.

**The four dispositions, borrowed unchanged from the gate sweep:**

| gate sweep | stamp gate | means |
|---|---|---|
| **act** | **rule** | Paul answers; the ruling goes in the block in his words |
| **fold** | **fold** | it is really another question / another item's — moved, with a pointer, not answered twice |
| **snooze** | **defer** | it does not block the step that is next; it re-surfaces when its named step is |
| **kill** | **withdraw** | it was never his — the agent can answer it from the record, or the option it offered is void |

⭐ **The snooze counter comes with it, and it is the anti-rot device.** The spine's rule —
*"the third snooze flags the row out loud; a timer pushed three times is an avoided decision wearing a
clock"* — reads for questions as: **a question deferred three times is an avoided decision wearing a
step number.** That is the mechanism that stops `.decisions/fernwood-5` (26 days, premise overtaken)
from happening inside plan files.

## 2 · WHAT MAKES A QUESTION ASKABLE — the bar, graded against real ones

**The bar is the `.decisions/` card, applied to a plan question. Three elements that already exist at
50/50, plus exactly ONE new key.**

> **A question is askable when it carries:**
> 1. **`options:`** — the closed set, `a | b | c`. *(Exists: 50/50 cards. Arity 2–4; 3 is the mode.)*
> 2. **a recommendation, or an explicit refusal to make one with its reason.** *(Exists: 50/50 cards
>    carry `### Recommendation`.)* ⚠️ See the warning below — **"always recommend" is wrong.**
> 3. **`blocks:`** — the step this question stops, by its own step id (`3a`, `1b`, `step 3`), or
>    **`blocks: none`**, which then requires **what stands until he rules**. ⭐ **The one new key.**
> 4. **it is actually his** — a question an agent could settle from the record is a **withdraw**, not
>    a question. *(No field; this is the drafting discipline, and §3's `withdraw` is where it is caught.)*

### Graded — four real batch-1 questions, on the bar

| question | options | recommendation | blocks | verdict | what it cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C5 Q1** *the module unit* | ✅ A or B | ✅ **B**, on one measurement (`turf` is not in `momlib.DOMAINS`) | ✅ *"3a cannot start without it"* — in prose | ⭐ **the exemplar** | ruled in three words: *"bundle makes sense."* |
| **Guru Q9** *where fixtures live* | ✅ tracked or local-only | ✅ `tools/guru-fixtures/` with the cap | ⚠️ implied (the replay leg) | ✅ askable | ruled in a batch of four |
| **C6 Q1** *`DEFAULT_SIZE` re-ruling* | ⛔ *"Yes / no"* — **a two-option frame that excluded the answer** | ⛔ none | ✅ *"1b cannot start without it"* | 🔴 **not askable as posed** | Paul invented a third answer; **1b/1c void, step 1 re-drafted, 158 measured lines of `viewer.html` in scope, one frozen question spawned** |
| **C7 Q4** *the AI-boundary third path* | ⛔ *"governed selection, or forbidden"*, no analysis | ⛔ *"Not C7's to rule; C7 only hands it off"* | ✅ its own item | ⚠️ **correctly a withdraw, filed as a question** | read by Paul at a gate where it could not be answered |

### ⚠️ THE WARNING THAT MUST TRAVEL WITH THE BAR — "always recommend" is FALSE

**Measured:** the two answers that came back **larger than the question** — C5 Q3 (→ the whole setup
journey) and C7 Q7 (→ frost as a three-layer engine rule) — were **both questions the seat declined to
recommend on.** Both produced product direction no recommendation would have surfaced.

So a recommendation is not a quality bar; it is a **declaration of what kind of question this is**,
and the two kinds want different handling:

| kind | carries | what it costs Paul | how it is answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| **assent** | a recommendation with its measurement | seconds | *"bundle makes sense" · "I trust your recommendation" · "Yeah"* — **and it batches** |
| **framing** | `no-recommendation:` + **why this one is his** (real-world context an agent cannot have) | minutes, and it is worth it | a paragraph that often reframes the item |

⛔ **A framing question with a fake recommendation on it is worse than no recommendation** — it invites
assent to a frame nobody tested. **A framing question with no stated reason for its absence is
indistinguishable from a lazy one**, and that is the failure C6 Q1 actually was: not "no
recommendation," but *no recommendation and no admission that the option set might be incomplete.*

⭐ **AND THE RECOMMENDATION IS THIS REPO'S COUNTERFACTUAL-CAPTURE DEVICE, which is a second reason to
want it.** This seat's founding audit measured that what leaks from the record is *the alternative
built, measured and rejected.* A recommendation written **before** the ruling means that when Paul
rules against it — C6 Q1, and the FOCUS FREEZE's two rejected defaults the same day — **the rejected
alternative is on disk, dated, with its reasoning, by construction.** Three preserved reversals in one
day, from a mechanism nobody built for that purpose. *(Overlaps the parallel C3 trace-query seat;
see §7 note.)*

### The one-line form, so this is replicable and not a framework

```
Q3 · assent · Mom's personId — opaque `p-…` or the handle `mom`?
   options: keep-mom-with-the-rule | mint-opaque-keep-mom-as-display
   recommend: mint-opaque — Q2's rule says an id is a coordinate, not a label
   blocks: 1b   (or: blocks: none — default: the resolver keeps minting `mom`)
```

Four lines, five with a caveat. It is the `.decisions/` card's own vocabulary, wrapped to fit a plan's
tail. **Nothing else is minted, and no template file is created.**

### ✅ TWO AMENDMENTS, ADOPTED `[paul-approved 2026-09-03]` — both from friction the first real run hit

> Paul, on the format's first live run (`.plans/2026-09-03-product-name-PLAN.md`): *"Go for both."*

Both came from **use, not theory** — the planning agent hit each of them drafting four real questions,
which is the only evidence this proposal wanted before changing its own bar.

**① One optional `caveat:` line.** The drafting agent found a case the four lines could not hold: a
recommendation that is *only honest with a reservation attached* — where the reservation is neither an
option, nor a reason-for-no-recommendation, nor a block. Its instance: the family label's identity-record
escalation, which is **reduced but not discharged** by the recommendation it rides on. Bolted into
`recommend:` it ran six lines and broke the four-line promise; as its own line it reads in one glance.

> **`caveat:` is for a reservation that survives the recommendation being taken.** If the reservation
> would change *which option you pick*, it is an option or a reason — not a caveat. ⚠️ It is **optional
> and rationed**: a caveat on every question is a drafter hedging, and the line stops meaning anything.

**② `blocks: stamp` is reserved vocabulary,** alongside a step id and `none`. Every worked example
blocked a *step*; the first real run produced a question that blocked **the item's own stamp** and had
to write `blocks: the stamp itself` — readable to a human, outside the vocabulary, and unresolvable if
anything ever parses the key. Three values now: **a step id · `stamp` · `none`** (and `none` still owes
*what stands until he rules*).

⭐ **Both amendments are evidence FOR the format, not against it** — the run reported that `blocks:`
did work nobody predicted (forcing each question to name the step it stops produced a reversibility
ladder that **inverted the item's apparent priority**), and that the assent/framing mark caught a live
instance of the exact C6 Q1 failure the format was written to stop. A bar that never bends on contact
with its first real item was not tested; these two bends are the test passing.

⚠️ **One friction was reported and deliberately NOT amended:** the run argued the assent/framing binary
hides a third kind — a **procedural** question about the *state of the evidence* rather than about the
product (its Q1: "does the re-run happen before the stamp?"). It has a recommendation and it batches, so
it is mechanically assent, but marking it assent reads as asking Paul to approve a design. **Left
unamended on purpose:** one instance is not a demonstrated need, and this proposal's own §2 was built by
refusing to mint vocabulary before the need was measured. Re-read it after the next batch — if a third
kind shows up again, that is the second instance and it earns a name.

## 3 · BATCHING AND SEQUENCING — thirty-three is not a conversation; eleven of them were not even due

**The sort is DERIVED from `blocks:`, never composed by hand.** That is the whole mechanism, and it is
why it is cheap.

1. **Gather** every question whose `blocks:` names a step that is **next or in flight**. In batch 1
   that would have put **~19 of 33** in front of Paul instead of 33 — the other ~14 by their own later
   text.
2. **Split the gathered set in two, and never mix them in one list:**
   - **assent block** — everything carrying a recommendation, presented together, answerable as a
     batch. Batch 1 proves this is real: *"Q5 · Q6 · Q7 ruled on the recommendations as written"* and
     *"if you want my stamp on the harness picture… I trust your recommendation."*
   - **framing block** — the `no-recommendation:` ones, presented **one at a time**, each with why it
     is his. Batch 1's two framing questions each produced a reframe; neither would have survived
     being item 7 of 10.
3. **Order the assent block by dependency, not by plan.** The queue's own closing note already
   derived it — *"The module UNIT (C5 Q1) is upstream of C5, C7 and Guru"* — and the ruling honoured
   it: C5 Q1 was ruled first and Guru Q10 was *discharged* by it without ever being asked. **An
   upstream question answered is downstream questions deleted.** That is the highest-leverage ordering
   there is, and it is derivable from the `blocks:` graph plus the plans' existing `depends-on:`.
4. **A deferred question re-surfaces at its step, and its defer count rides with it.** Third defer →
   flagged out loud (§1).

> ⭐ **The WIP rule does most of this work already — and it was seen working, twice, twenty minutes
> apart.** The declared default is *one item between concept and QA.* Two runs of
> `python3 tools/check-backlog-ready.py` while this file was being written:
>
> ```
> 21:40  🧭 In flight: c4-environments @ build · c6-door-for-paul @ build
>        🔴 WIP: 2 items between concept and qa and not every extra one carries `wip-exception:`
>           — the one-at-a-time default was crossed silently                          (exit 1)
>
> 22:0x  🧭 In flight: c4-environments @ build · c6-door-for-paul @ build (declared exception)
>        ✅ Readiness — 5 plan(s), every claim has its trail.                          (exit 0)
> ```
>
> The live session declared the exception between the two runs. **That is the mechanism doing exactly
> its job:** it never said the second item was wrong — a priority shift is legitimate and is Paul's —
> it said the shift was *undeclared*, and the response was to declare it. **Reported, not judged.**
>
> The method point stands either way: **if one item is in flight, the stamp gate is small by
> construction.** A ready-inventory question and a blocking question genuinely are different animals,
> and `blocks:` + the WIP rule are already the two instruments that separate them. Nothing new is
> needed.
>
> ⚠️ **And both readings are true, which is why the timestamp is on each.** A count quoted without the
> moment it was taken is the failure this repo names most often.

**Falsifier for §3:** if the `blocks:` field, once written, sorts fewer than a third of a batch's

## 4 · HOW A RULING IS CAPTURED — batch 1 already did this well; write it down before it drifts

**What batch 1 did, 5 of 5, and it should simply become the rule:** a blockquote inserted **above** the
question list in the plan's own `## Open before stamping`, carrying

1. **`✅ STAMPED <date> [paul-approved]`** — the stamp itself;
2. **Paul's words, verbatim, in quotes** — *"let's go with the canon that we established"*, *"my home
   dot place is a little cleaner"*, *"we will keep it very minimal right now"*;
3. **per-question rulings**, naming which were ruled **on the recommendation as written** and which
   were ruled **differently** (C6 Q1 says so explicitly);
4. **the consequences**, where a ruling voids build steps (C6 Q1 lists 1b/1c void, 1b′ substituted,
   `M3 retires by construction`);
5. **what is still open and where it now lives** — *"still open at their named steps."*

⭐ **The questions are NOT deleted when ruled.** They stay below the block. That is what preserves the
option that was not taken, and it costs nothing.

**Which sink, and do not double-file:**

| sink | when |
|---|---|
| **the plan file's ruling block** | **the default.** The ruling is about *this item's build*. It travels with the item and dies with it |
| **`.decisions/<project>-<n>.md`** | the question **outlives its item** or has no item — C7 Q4 (the AI-boundary third path) is exactly this and was correctly handed off |
| **`operating-layer/data/decisions.jsonl`** | the **event** — when a card is answered. Never edit status into a card file (`CLAUDE.md` § D33) |
| **`~/.claude/practice-principles/` etc.** | only when a ruling generalises beyond Fernwood (C7 Q7's frost ruling is a candidate — it became an **engine** rule mid-answer) |

⚠️ **OVERLAP DECLARED, NOT RESOLVED.** A parallel `practice-steward` seat is writing

## 5 · WHERE IT ATTACHES — the procedure-vs-loop tension, resolved rather than sidestepped

**The tension is real and both sides are Paul's own rulings.**

| | |
|---|---|
| readiness proposal §2 | *"Both read at pickup; neither fires a lap. **A procedure, not a loop** — nothing here can be owed."* §3: *"Does not add a state to any loop, beat to any map, or lap to anything."* |
| `CLAUDE.md` § backlog drift | a rationalization **beat** inside the mom cycle *"would quietly convert it into a backlog-driven loop and make grooming wait on Mom's cadence"* |
| against | Paul now asks for something **replicable and distributable across cycles** |
| and | ENACTMENT AMENDMENT: *"A standard travels on the **execution path**, not the reference path"* — conformance sections appended below beat tables reached nothing |

### The resolution: it is a PROCEDURE that a loop INVOKES, and the carrier is the artifact, not the map

**A beat would be wrong, and the reason is mechanical rather than aesthetic.** A beat fires on the
loop's cadence. The stamp gate's trigger is *"a step is next and a question blocks it"* — which is
**the item's clock, not the loop's.** Siting it as a beat would make a question wait for Mom's next
arrival at Fernwood, or for the fleet's next season signal. That is the exact conversion Paul ruled
against, one artifact over.

**So nothing is added to any map or any lap. Three things carry it instead, in ascending order of
reach:**

1. **THE ARTIFACT CARRIES IT.** A question carries `blocks:` and either a recommendation or
   `no-recommendation:`. This is checkable by a deterministic tool over files that already exist
   (`.plans/*-PLAN.md` and `.decisions/*.md`) and **requires no loop to change anything.** ⭐ This is
   the primary carrier precisely *because* it does not depend on any lap running.
2. **THE GATE SWEEP DISPOSES IT.** Where a loop opens with a gate sweep, its fired item-gates already
   include these. **No new step; the questions simply become visible to a sweep that already exists.**
3. **THE CHECK REPORTS IT.** `tools/check-backlog-ready.py` is already read in `CLAUDE.md`'s
   session-start block, already silent at zero, already counts in-flight items. A coverage line
   (§6) is a small addition to a tool that is already on the execution path.

> ⛔ **AND HERE IS WHY LAYER 2 CANNOT BE THE PRIMARY CARRIER — measured 2026-09-03, and it is the
> enactment amendment's own residue still standing.** Grep across `~/.claude/skills/`:
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | skills mentioning **"gate sweep"** | **1** — `health-cycle/SKILL.md` |
> | skills referencing `CYCLE-SPINE.md` | **3** — `health-cycle`, `identity-cycle`, `mom-cycle` |
>
> The spine carrier has grown since 2026-09-01 (the amendment's text says *"only one skill"*; it is
> now three — **read that count from a grep, not from this line**). **The gate sweep has not.** So a
> standard sited only on the gate sweep would reach **1 of the 4 lap-running skills** today, and would
> read as adoption-in-progress while reaching nothing — which is the precise failure the enactment
> amendment was written about. **Siting it on the artifact is what makes it reachable on day one.**

**Falsifier for §5:** if a loop adopts `blocks:` on its questions and its questions still pile up
unruled, the attachment point is wrong — the problem was never legibility and this design is treating
a symptom.

### What is being distributed

**One key and one reuse, no ceremony:**
